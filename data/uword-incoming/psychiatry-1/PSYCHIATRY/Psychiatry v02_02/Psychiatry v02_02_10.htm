A 13-year-old girl comes to the office with her mother due to concentration difficulties for the past month.  The patient recently started at a new school and has been taking much longer to read through assignments and finish her homework.  When called on by the teacher in class, her mind "goes blank," and she is often unable to remember what she wanted to say.  Although she has maintained average grades, the patient is concerned her recent lack of attention will cause her to fail out of school and not attend college.  The mother says, "I have to constantly remind her to calm down and focus because she gets wound up so easily about everything and thinks the worst is going to happen.  She's been like that since she was little."  Vital signs are within normal limits; growth curves are tracking consistently.  Physical examination shows no abnormalities.  On mental status examination, the patient is cooperative and friendly but sometimes fidgets in her seat.  Which of the following is the most appropriate pharmacotherapy for this patient?

 A.
Hydroxyzine
 B.
Methylphenidate
 C.
Propranolol
 D.
Sertraline
 E.
Tetrabenazine

Generalized anxiety disorder in children

Clinical features

  • Excessive, uncontrollable worry (multiple issues) ≥6 months
  • ≥1 of the following symptoms:
    • Restlessness; feeling on edge
    • Fatigue
    • Difficulty concentrating
    • Irritability
    • Muscle tension
    • Sleep disturbance

Associated features

  • Physical symptoms: stomachaches, headaches
  • Perfectionism

Treatment

  • Cognitive-behavioral therapy
  • SSRIs or SNRIs

Differential diagnosis

  • Adjustment disorder (response to identifiable stressor)
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder (intrusive thoughts; compulsive behaviors)
  • Separation anxiety disorder (anxiety focused on separation from caregiver)
  • Social anxiety disorder (fears of negative evaluation in social/performance situations)

SNRIs = serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor; SSRIs = selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor.

This patient's concentration difficulties associated with chronic worry, tension, and restlessness are suggestive of generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), a common anxiety disorder of childhood and adolescence.  Excessive worrying may frequently cause cognitive impairment, including feeling the mind is going blank, easy distractibility, and indecisiveness.  Catastrophic fears of the worst-case scenario, muscle tension, and physical restlessness (eg, fidgeting, leg shaking) are characteristic features.  Adolescents with GAD are often perfectionistic and worry excessively about academic performance.

When GAD symptoms are severe and impair social and academic functioning, pharmacotherapy is appropriate and is typically combined with cognitive-behavioral therapy.  Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (eg, sertraline) are considered the first-line pharmacotherapy for GAD in children and adolescents.

(Choice A)  Although the sedating antihistamine hydroxyzine can be used short-term for situational anxiety in children (eg, preoperative anxiety), it is not considered a first-line treatment for pediatric GAD.

(Choice B)  The stimulant methylphenidate is a first-line pharmacotherapy for attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), which can cause impaired concentration and hyperactivity (eg, fidgeting) in different settings (eg, school, home).  However, symptoms of impaired concentration must be present prior to age 12; in contrast, this patient has recent challenges with concentration (likely related to anxiety from starting at a new school) associated with a longstanding history of chronic anxiety ("thinks the worst is going to happen," "since she was little"), which is more consistent with GAD.

(Choice C)  The beta blocker propranolol is used in the treatment of performance anxiety in adults.  This patient's excessive worries are not limited to performing/speaking and being evaluated by others and are more consistent with GAD.

(Choice E)  Tetrabenazine, a vesicular monoamine transporter 2 inhibitor, has been used in the treatment of Tourette syndrome, a neurologic disorder manifested by motor and phonic tics with childhood onset.

Educational objective:
Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) is a common anxiety disorder in children and adolescents characterized by persistent and excessive worry and tension.  When GAD symptoms are severe and impairing, treatment with a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor combined with cognitive-behavioral therapy is indicated.
