Case Summary

Differential: Gestational Hypertension, Chronic hypertension, chronic renal disease, ITP, TTP, HUS, antiphospholipid syndrome.

The patient has pregnancy induced hypertension (PIH) (also known as gestational hypertension). PIH is defined as a new onset of hypertension (systolic > 140 and diastolic > 90) in the absence of proteinuria or new signs of
end-organ dysfunction. Severe PIH is defined as a systolic blood pressure > 160 or diastolic blood pressure > 110. This patient has severe PIH. The patient should be treated with antihypertensive medications until the baby
can be delivered. With severe PIH, the baby should be delivered at 34 weeks (2 weeks away in this case). The medications of choice are methyldopa, labetalol, or nifedipine. (Hydralazine is not recommended due to reflex
tachycardia). OB/GYN should be consulted as they will have to deliver the baby early. Magnesium Sulfate should also be administered for peripartum seizure prophylaxis in patients with severe PIH and for neuroprotection of
the fetus (even though she currently does not have pre-eclampsia). Once the antihypertensive agents are ordered, the case will end.

We have received emails stating that, according to the ACOG Bulletin 222 dated June 2020, pre-eclampsia with severe features can be diagnosed based on blood pressure alone. However, if you read the article, it lists the
diagnostic criteria for pre-eclampsia with severe features in Box 2, which DOES include proteinuria. Buried within the article, it then states that you can diagnose pre-eclampsia by blood pressure alone if systolic blood

pressure is > 160 or diastolic blood pressure is > 110.

In our opinion, this is intentionally contradictory information, as the authors do not wish to commit to changing the diagnostic criteria. This is still debated territory, and the USMLE typically does not involve material that is

still under debate. Therefore, for the purposes of the USMLE, we recommend using the traditional definition that pre-eclampsia requires proteinuria for a diagnosis.
