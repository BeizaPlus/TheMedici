Case Summary

Differential: Typical pneumonia, Lung cancer, Lung abscess, Bronchitis, Pulmonary embolus, Legionella pneumonia, COPD exacerbation, and Bronchiectasis.

This patient has Legionella pneumonia (also known as Legionnaires disease which is in the Atypical pneumonia category). This is a real scenario with an outbreak of Legionella due to a contaminated air conditioning system at
a convention, The clue is in the history where his other colleagues are also sick. Legionella pneumonia should be high on the differential as outbreaks can occur through contaminated water sources. Patients with Legionella

pneumonia also tend to present with Gl symptoms of nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea.

After doing a physical exam (which reveals lung findings consistent with a consolidation), basic labs should be obtained, including sputum culture and gram stain, as well as a chest X-ray. The patient is young without
comorbidities, so this case can be handled as an outpatient. The fastest way to get a positive test would be to test for the Legionella urine antigen. Although it can be falsely negative, if the Legionella urine antigen is positive,
then this would confirm the diagnosis. Legionella urine antigens can be detected as early as one day after symptom onset and can be detected from days to weeks. A typical culture can also be done, but the ideal testing
would be the Legionella urine antigen.

‘Once the diagnosis is made, the patient should be treated with antibiotics. The first line treatment is Levofloxacin or Azithromycin. Alternative antibiotics include: Moxifloxacin, Clarithromycin, Erythromycin, Ciprofloxacin,

Doxycycline. Once the patient has been treated with antibiotics, he will begin to feel better, and the case will end.
