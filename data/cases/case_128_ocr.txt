Case Summary
Differential: Physiologic hypercortisolism, Adrenal Tumor, Metastatic Disease, Exogenous Cortisol use.

The patient in this case has Cushing Disease. The symptoms the patient is suffering include: weight gain, plethora face (moon face), hirsutism (facial hair growth), thinning hair, and depression. Dexamethasone test is the standard test to test for Cushings syndrome. 24-hour urinary cortisol can also be used. The new test which is not currently on the USMLE is the late-night salivary cortisol test.

Once Cushings Syndrome has been established, the cause of it should be determined. This is best by measuring the ACTH. If the ACTH is high (above 20 pg/mL), then the cushings syndrome is ACTH-dependent. This could be caused by a pituitary tumor, ectopic ACTH, or corticotropin-releasing hormone secretion. The patient should have a brain MRI done to look for a pituitary tumor.

If the ACTH is low (below 5 pg/mL), then this would be an ACTH-independent disease. If this were the case, a CT of the adrenal glands should be done to look for an adrenal mass.

In this case, the ACTH is high. An MRI of the brain should also be done to look for a pituitary tumor. In this case, a pituitary tumor is found. Neurosurgery should be consulted to remove the tumor. No points would be given for treatment for depression like SSRIs or a consult to mental health. The treatment is not mental health services; it is fixing the underlying condition causing her depression.
