Differential: ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction, Acute Aortic Dissection, Pulmonary embolism, Pericardial tamponade, Esophageal rupture

This case is similar to case 124 and 125 but the presentation is different. The patient does not present with chest pain. She presents with atypical symptoms of shortness of breath and fatigue with some dizziness. This atypical
presentation is more common in elderly women and with patients who have diabetes and hypertension. The patient has ST depression in leads |, II, V4, VS, V6. This suggests a subendocardial ischemia.

Just like in case 124/125, the treatment is reperfusion. Cardiology should be consulted and an angioplasty with a stent placement should be performed (now known as percutaneous coronary intervention). This is the
preferred treatment. In the USMLE, you will have access to immediate treatment. Patients with Unstable angina or NSTEMI do not benefit from fibrinolysis. The patient should be given ticagrelor or prasugrel (Prasugrel is not
yet an order on the USMLE) as anticoagulants. Ticagrelor and prasugrel are preferred to Clopidogrel, but Clopidogrel will still be accepted as this knowledge is out of scope for the test.

Aspirin and anticoagulants should be given. Beta-blockers should be given. Morphine and nitroglycerin are not necessary as the patient does not complain of pain. Anticoagulants should also be given (UFH, Bivalirudin,
‘enoxaparin, or fondaparinux). Once treatment has taken place, the patient will feel better, and the case will end,
