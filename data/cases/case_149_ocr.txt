Case Summary

Differential: Antral G-cell hyperplasia, Retained gastric antrum, Gastric outlet obstruction, Renal failure, idiopathic gastroesophageal reflux, peptic ulcer disease, Helicobacter pylori infection.

The patient has Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome which is caused by a gastrin secreting tumor located in the duodenum or pancreas. Men are more likely to be diagnosed with Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome than women. Most of

these tumors occur spontaneously, but some come from those with MEN type | (Multiple endocrine neoplasia type I).

Diarrhea is one of the most common symptoms of Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome. Excess gastrin concentrations inhibit the absorption of water and sodium by the small intestine which causes diarrhea. Other common symptoms
of Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome are: abdominal pain, heartburn, nausea, vomiting, weight loss, and bleeding.

A serum gastrin value greater than 10 times the upper limit of normal (1000 pg/ml) is diagnostic of Zollinger-Ellison syndrome. The patient in this case has gastrin levels of 1,200 pg/mL.

An upper-Gl should be done to look for the tumor and to get a biopsy sample. A CT scan can be done to look for metastasis as gastrinomas can be malignant. Surgery should be consulted to remove the gastrinoma as

spontaneous gastrinomas can be cured by surgical removal of the tumor. The patient should be started on a PPI (ie. omeprazole) to limit complications of peptic ulcer disease.

‘Once the tumor is removed, the patient should be followed up by routine CT scans to check for any further occurrences. Screenings for parathormone levels, ionized calcium levels, and prolactin levels should be done to
screen for MEN1 tumors. The patient should also be counseled to stop smoking.
