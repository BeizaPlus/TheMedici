Case Summary

Differential: Otitis Externa, Ear Trauma, Foreign Body

The patient in this case has Acute Otitis Media. This is a very common ailment in children which is caused by a bacterial infection in the middle ear fluid. The most common presentation is ear pain and hearing loss. The
classical physical exam finding on exam is a bulging tympanic membrane. The bulging happens when there is middle ear effusion and inflammation which pushes the tympanic membrane.

This is mainly a treatment case as the main required diagnostic order is a HEENT physical exam.
First line treatment for otitis media in children is Amoxicillin or Amoxicillin/Clavulanate.

Second line treatments for otitis media in children are: Cefdinir, Cefpoxime, Cefuroxime, Ceftriaxone, Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Clindamycin, and Levofloxacin. These are not ideal for a simple case of otitis media and
points would be lost if these antibiotics were chosen,

The patient should be given acetaminophen or ibuprofen for pain, and the patient should be counseled about medication compliance to complete the entire antibiotic regimen to avoid antibiotic resistance and reinfection.
