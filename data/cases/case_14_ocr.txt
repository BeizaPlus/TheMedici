Case Summary

Pre-existing Hypertension, Antiphospholipid Syndrome, Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP), Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP), Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS), Hemolysis Elevated Liver Enzymes Low

The patient presents with symptoms that suggest Pre-Eclampsia with severe features — her initial blood pressure is 189/99, and she complains of increased frequency of headaches and new-onset blurry vision, both potential
signs of end-organ damage.

Pre-Eclampsia is defined as a systolic blood pressure of > 140 and/or a diastolic blood pressure of >90 that develops after 20 weeks gestation, and presents with a form of end-organ dysfunction. This has classically been
known to be proteinuria, but other symptoms that represent end-organ damage include:

+ Visual or cerebral disturbances
+ Pulmonary edema

+ Creatinine >1.1 mg/dL or 2 times patient's baseline

+ Elevated liver enzymes or severe RUQ pain

+ Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/uL)

+ Proteinuria - >300 milligrams in 24 hours, or Protein/Creatinine ratio of >0.3

As the patient is presenting with a systolic of greater than > 160, the patient meets the criteria of pre-eclampsia with severe features. The patient should be stabilized with magnesium sulfate as seizure prophylaxis,
antihypertensive medication (first line - IV labetalol and hydralazine), and antenatal glucocorticoids to progress fetal lung maturation if delivering <34 weeks.

Delivery of the baby is the definitive treatment of preeclampsia and eclampsia. In general, the following rules apply to the induction of delivery of patients with preeclampsia:
+ With severe features: deliver =34 weeks

+ Without severe features: deliver =37 weeks

Key components of the pre- and intrapartum management of preeclampsia include:

+ Antenatal glucocorticoids (betamethasone)

+ Magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis

+ Antihypertensive therapy (if =160/110 mmHg)

If the pregnancy is greater than or equal to 34 weeks of gestation, delivery is recommended. If the pregnancy is less than 34 weeks and the maternal and fetal status is stable, a course of corticosteroids followed by delivery is
recommended. If the pregnancy is less than 30 to 32 weeks with an unfavorable cervix, cesarean section is recommended to avoid prolonged induction.
