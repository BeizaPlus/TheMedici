Case Summary

Differential: Diverticulitis, Gastroenteritis, Infectious colitis, Irritable bowel syndrome, Ischemic colitis, Lactose intolerance, Pseudomembranous colitis, Small bowel lymphoma, Ulcerative colitis, Celiac disease, Small intestinal
bacterial overgrowth (SIBO).

This patient has Crohn's disease. He presented with typical symptoms including crampy abdominal pain and diarrhea and his labs indicated occult blood in the stool and elevated inflammatory markers. Diagnosis is made via
colonoscopy showing mucosal inflammation next to areas of normal mucosa (“skip lesions”) and linear ulcerations making a “cobblestone” appearance. These endoscopic features along with the characteristic involvement of
the terminal ileum and right colon with rectal sparing is indicative of Crohn's disease as opposed to Ulcerative Colitis. Once this is diagnosed, the patient should be started on budesonide or prednisone to improve symptoms
The use of 5-aminosalicylates such as mesalamine or sulfasalazine is less useful than it is in Ulcerative Colitis but can still be options for patients with limited ileitis and mild symptoms. Immunomodulators (azathioprine,
methotrexate, 6-MP) and anti-TNF agents (infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab pegol (certolizumab is not yet available on the USMLE) are for higher risk patients with moderate to severe Crohn's disease but would still be
accepted in this case. Because the main focus of this case is diagnosing the patient and ordering appropriate diagnostic tests based on the history and physical exam, a wide range of treatment orders would still be accepted.

Consulting Gl is also indicated, as they will be the team helping with these complicated treatment decisions.
