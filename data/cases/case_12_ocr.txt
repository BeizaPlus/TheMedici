Case Summary

Differential: Urethritis, Epididymitis, Gonorrhea, Syphilis, HIV, HSV.

Chlamydia is the most common bacterial STI. The organism is an intracellular pathogen. Patients who are contract chlamydia are often co-infected with gonorrhea (40% of women, 20% of men), so it is recommended to treat

for both pathogens empirically.

Cases of chlamydia are often asymptomatic, with 80% of women and 50% of men reporting no symptoms. In this case, the presence of purulent urethral discharge, dysuria, and scrotal swelling gives you clues that this patient
has contracted an infection.

It is important to screen for all STIs on this visit as the patient may have a co-infection of one or more of the STIs (Gonorrhea, HIV, Hep C, Hep B, syphilis, HSV). The Nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT), a DNA probe test on
first-catch urine, is first-line for the diagnosis of chlamydia, but a culture is also acceptable. Treatment should be empirically given, and the patient's partners should be treated as well.

Treatment for chlamydia is to give Azithromycin (oral one dose) or doxycycline (oral x 7 days).
