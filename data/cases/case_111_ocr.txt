Case Summary

Differential: Pulmonary Embolism, Air embolism, Aspiration, Heart failure, Pneumonia, Systemic lupus erythematosus.

The patient has fat embolism syndrome (FES). This typically occurs from hours to days to weeks after an initial injury that allows fat globules (typically from bone marrow) to enter the blood stream. Long bones such as a
femur are common causes of FES. FES, as in this case, can cause respiratory distress, and if intervention is not done quickly, then death soon will follow.

The patient has many metabolic abnormalities. He has both anemia and thrombocytopenia which are very common in fat embolism syndrome. He also has metabolic acidosis, increased level of BUN, and increased creatinine

which are also commonly seen in fat embolism syndrome.

Diagnosis is mainly clinical as imaging is not very reliable. Chest x-rays and CT scans can be normal as in this case. The patient would have an abnormal VQ scan, but this will take too much time to administer. The patient will
require immediate supportive treatment.

A commonly used criteria is the Gurd and Wilsons criteria for fat embolism to help clinicians determine if the patient is suffering from a fat embolism. The major criteria are:
Axillary or subconjunctival petechiae

Hypoxemia PaO2 <60 mm Hg

Central nervous system depression disproportionate to hypoxemia

Pulmonary edema

There are minor criteria, but these will not be listed. The pertinent positives in the minor criteria are Tachycardia more than 110 beats per minute, and fat globules present in the urine, At least two positive major criteria plus
‘one minor criteria is suggestive of fat embolism. In this case, the patient has axillary petechiae, hypoxemia, and tachycardia. Fat globules in the urine is not a current order on the USMLE.

Treatment is supportive. The patient should be transferred to the ICU, intubated, and put on a ventilator. The patient should be given fluids. Albumin therapy is considered experimental and corticosteroids administration is
controversial, so the mainstay treatment is just supportive. One thing to avoid is heparin/anticoagulant treatment. This has not shown benefit in fat embolism cases, and this increases the risk of hemorrhage.
