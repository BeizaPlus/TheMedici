Case Summary

Differential: Myocardial infarction, Hematuria, Myopathy
The patient has Rhabdomyolysis due to excessive exercise from running a marathon. The most common presentation is with muscle pain, weakness, and dark urine. In this case, the patient presents with all 3 symptoms. The
key lab value is an elevated CPK level. Inflammation of the liver is also common in Rhabdomyolysis. On urinalysis, the patient will have positive blood but will have few RBC on microscopic examination as myoglobin will make

the dipstick positive.

The treatment for Rhabdomyolysis is fluids. There is no agreed upon regimen, so normal saline, 0.45% normal saline, or lactate ringer can be administered. The patient should then be given bicarbonate to alkalinize the urine

to prevent conversion of myoglobin to methemoglobin. Fluids and waiting are the only effective treatment, Hemodialysis is not necessary in this patient.
