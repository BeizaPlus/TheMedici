Case Summary

Differential: Bowel Obstruction, Functional dyspepsia, Bulimia
The patient in this case has Diabetic Gastroparesis. This is caused by diabetic neuropathy of the autonomic system causing decreased motility of the stomach. This can happen in both type | and type II diabetes.

First line treatment for gastroparesis is Metoclopramide. Metoclopramide (Reglan) is both an antiemetic and a prokinetic and is FDA approved to treat gastroparesis for up to 12 weeks (This can be longer if the patient is
willing to take on the risks of tardive dyskinesia).

Metoclopramide is a D2 blocker, so it comes with the risk of tardive dyskinesia (this is like taking haloperidol for an extended period of time). Domperidone is used in the UK and Canada, but this is not available in the US and
is not an order on the USMLE.

Second line treatment for gastroparesis is Erythromycin or Azithromycin. Both of these antibiotics increase gastric emptying.

The patient's increased glucose should be treated. The patient should be put on insulin therapy due to the frequent vomiting. It is unlikely he is getting much efficacy from oral medications. He can be trialed to be
transitioned back to oral medications once his nausea and vorniting is controlled.

Glucose control can help gastroparesis symptoms, but the damage done by prolonged hyperglycemia could be a permanent issue for the patient. However, glucose control should be implemented regardless.

In this case, the patient will feel better after given Metoclopramide.
