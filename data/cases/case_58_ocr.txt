Case Summary

Differential: Bacterial tracheitis, Diphtheria, Epiglottitis, Measles, Peritonsillar abscess, Retropharyngeal abscess, foreign body aspiration, anaphylaxis.

This patient has croup (laryngotracheitis) most likely from a respiratory virus. She has the classical findings of croup including a barking cough and respiratory stridor in the setting of recent upper respiratory symptoms (nasal
congestion) in the past 12-48 hours. Although croup can be diagnosed clinically with history and physical exam alone, a neck X-ray in this case would show subglottic narrowing and clinch the diagnosis. Because this patient
has stridor at rest and intercostal retractions, she should be sent to the emergency department for further management in case her respiratory status deteriorates. No further diagnostic testing needs to be done. In the ED the

patient should receive nebulized epinephrine, dexamethasone (either PO, IM, or IV whichever the patient can tolerate), antipyretics, and IV fluids. Once the patient is given the epinephrine and dexamethasone, the patient will
feel better and can be observed until the morning and discharged home.
