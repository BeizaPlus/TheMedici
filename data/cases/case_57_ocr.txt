Case Summary

Differential: Metabolic disease, Enterocolitis, Small bowel obstruction, Volvulus, Incarcerated inguinal hernia, Appendicitis, Meckel's diverticulum, Gastroenteritis.

This patient has intussusception (likely ileo-colic, the most common type accounting for 90% of all cases) or telescoping of his intestines. This is the most common cause of intestinal obstruction in children between 6-36
months of age and is a medical emergency, as it can be fatal if not treated promptly. The patient has a classic presentation of intermittent abdominal pain, vorniting, bloody stool, lethargy, and a right-sided abdominal mass.
The patient should be admitted to the hospital and receive IV fluid resuscitation for his dehydration. Abdominal x-ray will demonstrate evidence of bowel obstruction with a R-sided mass (but without evidence of perforation),
and ultrasound will further characterize the mass as a classic radiographic “target sign’, further clinching the diagnosis of intussusception. An NG tube should be placed to help decompress the obstructed bowel and the
patient should be made NPO. Although pediatric surgery should be consulted and will follow the patient, first-line treatment for intussusception in a clinically stable patient without signs of perforation or shock is

nonoperative reduction via barium or air enema. In this case, nonoperative reduction is successful and the case will end at this time.
