Case Summary

Differential: Acute intermittent porphyria, Variegate porphyria, Hereditary coproporphyria, Congenital erythropoietic porphyria, Erythropoietic protoporphyria, X-linked protoporphyria, ALA dehydratase porphyria, Liver
disease

The patient in this case has Porphyria cutanea tarda (PCT). PCT is caused due to reduce activity of uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase. Reduced hepatic uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase activity leads to hepatic accumulation of
highly carboxylated porphyrinogens which are then transported to the skin which then cause damage when exposed to light. The light (near 400nm) activate oxygen in the tissues that damages proteins, basement
membranes, and lipids which causes the skin lesions and blistering.

Biopsies are not necessary in PCT. The diagnosis can be made through laboratory studies and clinical symptoms.

There are a variety of porphyrias that are on the differential. For the scope of the USMLE, the main differential to rule out is Acute intermittent porphyria (AIP). PCT will have normal urine delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) levels

and normal porphobilinogen (PBG) levels while AIP will have elevated ALA and PBG levels. AIP will also have a port-wine color urine and can have recurrent bouts of severe abdominal pain.

A common cause of PCT is infection with the Hepatitis C virus. Although the patient has mildly elevated LFTs, the patient should be treated for Hepatitis C. This can be done by referring to infectious disease. The treatment
protocol for treating Hepatitis C is outside the scope of the USMLE, so an infectious disease referral is sufficient.

The treatment for PCT is Phlebotomy or low-dose hydroxychloroquine (or chloroquine). As sunlight is a factor that causes the blistering, the patient should be advised to avoid exposure to sunlight until porphyrin levels have

normalized. The patient should also be advised to avoid alcohol as this can exacerbate PCT.

In real life, it can take up to 6 months to get normal porphyrin concentrations with treatment. In this case, the patient will improve after a couple weeks of treatment.
