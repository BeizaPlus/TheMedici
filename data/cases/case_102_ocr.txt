The classic lab diagnostic criteria for insulinoma is low blood glucose along with elevated plasma insulin, elevated C-peptide, and elevated proinsulin. Proinsulin is not yet on the USMLE, so this is not required. insulinoma is
very rare in real life, but it’s not rare on the USMLE as this is frequently seen on the test. This case is not how you would typically diagnose insulinoma. The gold standard diagnosis is 2 72 hour fast following by drawing
insulin, C-peptide, and proinsulin labs. A 72-hour fasting order is not available on the USMLE, so this is not an option. Enough information is available to make the diagnosis without a 72 hour fasting test, but ordering a 72
hour fasting test is what would typically be done in real life. In this case, the patient has been fasting for 14 hours, so the lab data will be accurate enough to lead to imaging to look for an insulinoma, Abdominal ultrasound
or CT / MRI of the abdomen is appropriate. Contrast may or may not help in the CT/MRI, so either is acceptable.

The key in this case is to not be satisfied that the patient feels better after initially making the patient feel better by administering glucose. After appropriately diagnosing the patient, general surgery will need to be consulted
‘to remove the tumor.

If the patient is not a surgical candidate or refuses surgery, then Diazoxide, Octreotide, Verapamil, or Phenytoin can be administered. These medications can help prevent symptomatic hypoglycemia.
