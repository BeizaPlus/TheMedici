Case Summary

Differential: Ectopic Pregnancy, Tubo-Ovarian Abscess, Appendicitis, Kidney Stones, Ruptured Ovarian Cyst.

The patient in this case has Ovarian Torsion. This is a relatively rare surgical emergency. The most common presentation is pelvic pain with nausea and vomiting. A pelvic mass is a risk factor for ovarian torsion (i. cyst,

dermoid). The patient has a 7cm cyst with decrease blood flow.

Ovarian torsion is caused by rotation of the infundibulopelvic ligament which restricts blood flow causing pain and eventually necrosis if surgical intervention is not quickly performed. The treatment for ovarian torsion is

surgical detorsion (assuming the ovary is not necrotic).

The patient's symptoms of having cramping over the last few days was likely due to the ovary twisting and untwisting repeatedly.

OB/GYN should be consulted for a laparoscopic detorsion.
