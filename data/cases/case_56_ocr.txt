Case Summary

Differential: Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, DIC, Sickle cell anemia, Hereditary spherocytosis, Thalassemias, TTP, Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria.

This patient has glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency. The patient had a severe reaction to the oxidative effect of Primaquine, an anti-malarial agent. Ideally, G6PD deficiency would be tested prior to initiating
Primaquine, but the patient was treated in a local hospital in Africa which likely had limited resources to run this test. The patient presented with signs and symptoms consistent with an acute hemolytic anemia including
jaundice, fatigue, darkened urine, and abdominal pain. Lab findings showed a severe anemia (hemoglobin of 6.8 and hematocrit of 21) with other findings confirming an intravascular hemolytic process (indirect
hyperbilirubinemia, high LDH, high reticulocyte count, low haptoglobin, hemoglobinuria). Peripheral blood smear showing bite cells practically clinches the diagnosis of G6PD deficiency along with the history of taking
Primaquine. However, testing for G6PD deficiency during an acute hemolytic episode is not recommended, as it can yield falsely negative results, so this patient should be treated for the acute hemolytic anemia and then
followed up with testing ~3 months later and advised to avoid potential sources of oxidant injury. For this patient, treatment should involve immediately stopping Primaquine, resuscitating with IV fluids and packed RBCs in
the hospital, and treating the patient's pain appropriately. Once the patient is successfully treated, you can counsel him and his family about the diagnosis and to avoid certain foods/medications then discharge him home
with appropriate follow-up in 1-3 months, at which time testing for G6PD deficiency would be indicated,
