Case Summary

Differential: Hyperparathyroidism, Excessive Opioid use, Effects of Metastatic Cancer

Hypercalcemia is common in patients with metastatic cancer. The mechanism of hypercalcemia secondary to malignancy can be due to a number of factors. It could be due to the tumor secreting parathyroid hormone-
related protein, due to bone metastasis causing local release of cytokines causing release of calcium, or it could be due to the tumor production calcitriol. It could also be a combination of these factors.

The patient can be put in any location except the ICU as the patient does not wish for extreme intervention. The best first step is to get a PTH to see if it's is low. If itis low such as in this case, this means that it is being
suppressed by tumor producing parathyroid hormone-related protein.

The patient has somnolence due to severe hypercalcemia with a calcium level of 14. The treatment for severe hypercalcemia of malignancy is to treat with IV fluids, calcitonin, and a bisphosphonate. It will take several days for
‘the bisphosphonate to be effective (typically day 2-4).

After the first 48 hours, calcitonin should be stopped and the bisphosphonate should be continued. Calcitonin will stop being effective due to receptor downregulation, but it is the short-term treatment that can help
hypercalcemia while waiting for the longer acting bisphosphonate to start working.

The bisphosphonate of choice in hypercalcemia of malignancy is Zoledronic acid, but Pamidronate, Ibandronate, and other bisphosphonates can also be used.
