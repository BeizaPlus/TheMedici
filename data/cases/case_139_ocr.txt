Case Summary

The patient has squamous cell carcinoma of the lip. This is a fairly straight forward case. You diagnose by a biopsy and then remove the tumor by consulting dermatology to perform Mohs surgery. Mohs surgery tends to be

the gold standard for removing facial tumors as it minimizes tissue loss.

The Mohs surgeon will remove a layer of area where the tumor was located and take the tissue, dye it, and look at the histology of the removed tissue. If there are still cancer cells in the specimen, the Mohs surgeon will go

back and remove more of the lesion until there is no more cancer. The patient will wait in the office while the Mohs surgeon looks at the tissue, so that the surgeon can remove more tissue if necessary. Mohs surgery will

preserve the most tissue and have the best cosmetic outcome which is important to most patients.

Mohs surgery is an outpatient procedure and can be done with local anesthetic. Once the surgery is completed, the case will end.
