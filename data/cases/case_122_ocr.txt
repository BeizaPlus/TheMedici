Case Summary

Differential: Erythema multiforme, Generalized bullous fixed drug reaction, Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome, Linear IgA bullous dermatosis

The patient in this case has Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) from his exposure to Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole. Drug exposure is the most common cause of SJS. The next most common in children is a bacterial infection
with Mycoplasma pneumoniae. Because of this, it is recommended that tests for Mycoplasma pneumoniae to screen for this.

It is important to get bacterial and fungal cultures of the wounds because it is common that patients with SIS will have bacterial infection and sepsis.

The treatment for SJS is mainly supportive. The first thing to do would be to stop the offending agent which in this case would be the Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole. The patient should then be admitted to the ICU and be

given IV fluids and pain medication.

As this patient is presenting in the initial stages of SJS, Cyclosporine should be given as it has been shown to help slow progress of SIS in early stages.
