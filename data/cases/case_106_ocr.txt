Case Summary

Differential diagnosis: Transfusion reaction, Chemotherapy, Tumor related fever, Tumor lysis syndrome.

The patient is severely neutropenic. He has an ANC of 475 (calculated by taking 2500 and times by Bands + Segmented neutrophils (17% + 2%) = 2500 * 19% = 475). Severe neutropenia is an ANC < 500. The patient is at
severe risk and should be admitted immediately and started on empiric antibiotics. In a neutropenic patient, antibiotics should be administered within 60 minutes of presentation (some say as early as 30 minutes within
presentation). Experts have compared a neutropenic patient to be as serious as a myocardial infarction patient which requires swift and immediate treatment. The goal of this case is to teach how to quickly diagnose the

patient with severe neutropenia and then quickly administer antibiotics.

The source of the infection in this case will not be known. Only 20-30% of patients with chemotherapy induced neutropenia will have a known source of infection. In the other 70-80% of the cases, the presumption is that the
source of infection comes from the bacterial flora of the intestines. Although the case will end before the bacterial cultures will return, the culture would have been positive for Psuedomonas aeruginosa, so antipseudomonal
antibiotics will be required in the case. The antibiotic treatment recommended by the IDSA for empiric treatment for a neutropenic patient is monotherapy with one of the following antibiotics: Cefepime, Meropenem,
Imipenem, Piperacillin-tazobactam, or Ceftazidime. These have activity against Psuedomonas aeruginosa which in this case is the bacterial agent responsible for the patient's initial presentation.
