Case Summary
Differential: Carbon monoxide poisoning, Hydrogen sulfide gas poisoning, Phosphine poisoning
The patient has cyanide poisoning. The most common cause of cyanide poisoning in the United States is due to inhalation of smoke in residential or commercial fires. Cyanide disrupts the electron transport chain in the

mitochondrial production of ATP by binding to and inhibiting cytochrome oxidase a. As aerobic cellular metabolism stops, cells are forced into anerobic metabolism with the subsequent generation of lactate. This will cause
severe acidosis and very elevated lactate levels which can lead to cellular death.

Cyanide is produced in house fires due to combustion of materials such as rubber, wool, plastics, and synthetic materials.
The history suggests either a carbon monoxide poisoning or a cyanide poisoning. The hint in the history is that there is a dense acrid smoke that was protruding from the house. The patient will need to be intubated and put
‘ona ventilator. A fingerstick glucose, a CMP, an EKG, lactate, ABG, carboxyhemoglobin should be drawn, Lactate aids in differentiating between cyanide poisoning and carbon monoxide poisoning. If lactate is normal or mildly
elevated, then this would be more consistent with carbon monoxide poisoning alone.

As the carboxyhemoglobin is only 10%, along with severe acidosis and highly elevated lactate, the diagnosis of cyanide poisoning should be clear. The treatment of choice is Hydroxocobalamin. Hydroxocobalamin is the
hydroxylated active form of vitamin B12. Once Cyanide poisoning is suspected, an initial dose of Hydroxocobalamin should be given. If the patient does not quickly improve, then a second dose can be given. If the patient

does not improve then a third dose can be given along with sodium thiosulfate. Amyl/sodium Nitrite should be avoided as this can cause worsening oxygen delivery if the patient has concurrent carbon monoxide toxicity.

‘Once the patient is put on Hydroxocobalamin, the patient will begin to improve, and the case will end.
