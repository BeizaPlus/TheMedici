Case Summary

Differential diagnosis: Ruptured ectopic pregnancy, Endometriosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, Adnexal torsion, Appendicitis

The patient has a ruptured ovarian cyst. Ovarian cysts occur commonly in menstruating women aged 18-35 years and are commonly identified by ultrasound. Two types of functional ovarian cysts are follicular cysts and
corpus luteum cysts.

While most hemorrhage associated with ovarian cysts have an unclear etiology, there are some risk factors which include: anticoagulation therapy and abdominal trauma. The right ovary tends to rupture more often than the
left ovary.

Typically at the time of rupture, there may be sharp and sudden onset of pain, which typically does not have typical characteristics. Blood loss can vary from very little bleeding to hypovolemic shock as is in this case.

Ultrasonic evidence of large amounts of peritoneal fluid and severe pain is an indication for operative intervention as well as decreased hematocrit and unstable vital signs. The patient in this case required surgical

intervention although most patients only require observation and some analgesics.

There is no preventive treatment in this case.
