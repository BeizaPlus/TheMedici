Case Summary

Differential: Pelvic inflammatory disease, Urethritis, Vaginitis

The patient in this case has acute cystitis. This is a very common ailment in women due to the close proximity of women's urethra to the anus. This is a simple case of acute cystitis with the typical symptoms of dysuria, urinary

frequency, and urinary urgency. This would mean that the infection is confined to the bladder.

If the patient had fevers, chills, flank pain, or other symptoms of systemic infection, then you would suspect infection outside of the bladder. If this were the case, then pyelonephritis and complicated urinary tract infections

would be high on the differential.

This case is different than case #24 because in case #24, the patient is pregnant. A urinalysis should be done. When it comes back positive, the patient should be started on antibiotics.

First line antibiotics are Nitrofurantoin, Trimethoprim with or without sulfamethoxazole, and Fosfomycin.

Second line agents for acute cystitis are beta-lactams (Amoxicillin-clavulanate, Cefpodoxime, Cefdinir, Cefadroxil, Cephalexin). Points would be lost for using a second line agent for this simple case of acute cystitis

Third agents for acute cystitis are fluroquinolones (i. Ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin). These should be reserved for only serious cases. This would be inappropriate in a simple acute cystitis case. More points would be lost for

using fluroquinolones in this case.

Preventive care of a pap smear should be offered to the patient.
