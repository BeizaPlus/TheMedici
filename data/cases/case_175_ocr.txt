Case Summary
Differential: Von Willebrand disease (vWD), Thrombocytopenia, Hemophilia A, Platelet Abnormalities (Glanzmann thrombasthenia), Vasculopathies, Connective Tissue Diseases, Collagen Disorders, hypothyroidism.

The first step is to assess anemia and platelet count by getting a CBC. This shows anemia but normal platelets. Then order PT/PTT, revealing normal PT, mildly elevated PTT, and normal fibrinogen, pointing toward vWD. This is a classic presentation for vWD diagnosis in females around menarche due to heavy periods. Once suspected, order vWF assays showing decreased ristocetin-induced platelet aggregation at 1.0 mg/mL, suggestive of type 1 vWD. Confirmatory vWF activity takes 3 days, so the case ends before results, but initial findings allow empirical treatment.

Start IV fluids for hypovolemia. Give DDAVP (desmopressin, 0.3 mcg/kg IV) concurrently with tranexamic acid for acute bleeding control. DDAVP boosts endogenous vWF/Factor VIII, working within 30-60 minutes (peak at 1 hour), lasting 8-24 hours. It's safe for type 1; risks include hyponatremia and tachyphylaxis. Avoid in type 2B as it may worsen thrombocytopenia. If ineffective, use vWF concentrates.

Tranexamic Acid is typically started concurrently with DDAVP, not after it fails, to stabilize clots and enhance hemostasis, especially for mucosal bleeding like menorrhagia.

Preventive care would be to prevent future bleeds, so hormonal therapy such as oral contraceptives could be started or the patient could be referred to OB/GYN. Hormonal therapy helps by increasing vWF/factor VIII levels and stabilizing the endometrium. Counsel the patient on avoiding NSAIDs and trauma (playing sports). Add iron for anemia.
