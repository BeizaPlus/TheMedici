Case Summary

Differential: Hyperthyroidism, Hypothyroidism, Menopause, Pregnancy, Prolactinoma, Pheochromocytoma, Anxiety or Panic Disorder.

This patient is experiencing menopause. Hot flashes, a sudden heat sensation that is often generalized and associated with perspiration and occasionally palpitations, are the most common presenting symptom. The pain this
patient experiences during intercourse is due to vaginal dryness from decreased vaginal lubrication. All of these symptoms are due to decreased estrogen during menopause. Menopause is a clinical diagnosis, and does not
require confirmatory labs if patients are aged 45 years or older. Treatment options include: replacement estrogen with progesterone. There are risks inherit with this which include various cancers and blood clotting. Non-

hormonal pharmacotherapy includes SSRI/SNRIs (venlafaxine, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram) or gabapentin. Risks and benefits of each treatment option should be discussed with the patient. Some patients may opt to
choose no intervention and, simply, wait out the symptoms which may improve with time.
