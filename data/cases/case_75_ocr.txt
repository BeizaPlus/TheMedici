Case Summary

Differential: Pericardial tamponade, Myocardial infarction, Pericarditis, Pulmonary embolism, Esophageal rupture.

This patient has an ascending (type A) aortic dissection. This is a surgical emergency, and the case will test to see if you can make the diagnosis and get the patient immediately into emergency surgery. The patient's
symptoms of acute-onset, tearing chest pain radiating to the back in the setting of a history of hypertension in addition to exam findings of a new diastolic murmur (suggestive of aortic regurgitation) and systolic blood
pressure differential between right and left arms is highly concerning for ascending aortic dissection. The patient should be placed on a cardiac monitor, given supplemental oxygen and IV opioids for pain relief while an EKG
(to look for signs of ischemia) and chest x-ray are obtained. Initial imaging with a chest x-ray will show mediastinal widening, but definitive diagnosis requires either a transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE; preferred for
hemodynamically unstable patients or in those with signs/symptoms suggestive of ascending dissection) or a CT angiogram of the chest, which will show two distinct lumens within the aorta. Thoracic or cardiothoracic
surgery should be consulted immediately and the patient should then be given IV beta blockers (to lower heart rate and left ventricular velocity) followed by vasodilator therapy (to lower systemic blood pressure). Once
surgery has been consulted, the case will end.
