Case Summary

Differential: Inclusion body myositis, Myopathy due to drugs/toxins, Myotonic dystrophy, Polymyositis, Polymyalgia theumatica, Hypothyroidism, Fibromyalgia.

This patient has myopathy secondary to statin use. Although generally considered safe medications, statins are known to cause different muscle-related adverse effects, ranging from mild myalgias to myopathy to severe
myonecrosis leading to clinical rhabdomyolysis. Combining certain medications, such as a fibrate (Gemfibrozil), with a statin further increases the risk of myopathy. This patient reports subacute onset of symmetric, proximal
muscle weakness and soreness within several weeks of initiating simvastatin and gemfibrozil and he has exam findings consistent with these symptoms (tenderness to palpation and lower extremity proximal muscle
weakness). Basic lab workup should include CBC, electrolytes, creatine phosphokinase (CK), inflammatory markers, TSH, Vitamin D level, and urinalysis. Results will show a moderately elevated CK, supporting the diagnosis of
myopathy, but normal urinalysis, which is important to rule out rhabdomyolysis-induced kidney damage. The patient's simvastatin and gemfibrozil should be immediately discontinued, and no further testing is warranted at
this time. The patient should also be encouraged to hydrate with lots of fluids to prevent kidney damage from myoglobin (a complication of rhabdomyolysis), although his urinalysis at this time does not show evidence of
this. If the patient's symptoms do not resolve after several weeks, further testing with EMG or muscle biopsy may be necessary to look for other pathologies causing his muscle symptoms. In this case, the patient's symptoms
resolve after a few days (sometimes it can take weeks) and CK levels are trending down. After the patient has adequate time to recover, it is reasonable to trial another statin alone (without gemfibrozil), particularly

pravastatin, fluvastatin, or rosuvastatin, since these have fewer reported drug interactions compared with other statins.
