Case Summary

Differential: Beta-ketothiolase deficiency. Glycine encephalopathy, Methylmalonic acidemia, Propionic acidemia.

The patient has the signs of classic Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD) which include: irritability, poor feeding, bicycling movements, focal dystonia, and seizures. The are 5 types of MSUD (Classic, Intermediate, Intermittent,
Thiamine-responsive, and E3-deficient). Classic MSUD is the most common. The amino acid levels are given to you because these orders are not present on the USMLE at this time. There are no orders for serum or urine
levels of glutamic acid, valine, isoleucine, or leucine. However, you must put together that high amino acid levels with the presentation to come up with the MSUD diagnosis.

The treatment for MSUD is a no-protein/low-protein diet. This specialized diet would be life-long much like PKU. The patient would not be able to breast-feed and would need a non-branched-chain amino acids (meaning
low, leucine, valine, isoleucine) formula. There is no specific diet order on the USMLE for a non-branched-chain amino acid diet, so a low-protein/no protein diet will suffice. It is recommended that all patients with MSUD be
treated with a thiamine to see if the patient has the Thiamine-responsive type. If there is no improvement after 4 weeks, then the thiamine treatment can be discontinued.

A potential long-term treatment of MSUD is liver transplantation. As the patient is only a week old, this discussion would happen at a later time and would depend on the outcome of diet modification.

Note that in this case, the CBC/CMP will be normal which is not abnormal as the patient seems to be suffering more neurological damage than metabolic issues. However, metabolic decompensation is a possibility, so the
patient should be followed closely. Admitting to the hospital/ICU is appropriate until the patient can be stabilized and amino acid levels returned to normal levels. MSUD is more severe than PKU as this can lead to death

The patient will have protein and ketones in the urine with an elevated serum ammonia and CRP.
