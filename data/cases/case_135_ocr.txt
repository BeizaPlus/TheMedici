Case Summary

Differential: Guillain-Barre syndrome, Hyperkalemic Periodic Paralysis, Pseudohypoaldosteronism

The patient has Hyperkalemia due to ACE Inhibitor Use and NSAID use. Hyperkalemia can manifest with symptoms which range from asymptomatic to having: nausea, vomiting, areflexia, weakness, flaccid paralysis,
paresthesias, and intestinal colic. ECG changes can include: tall, peaked T waves, wide QRS, PR prolongations, and a loss of P waves. This can progress to sine waves, ventricular fibrillation, and cardiac arrest.

ACE inhibitors will block angiotensin I! which will reduce aldosterone secretion which will reduce potassium secretion which will then raise the potassium serum concentration.
NSAIDs can lower renal renin secretion and impair angiotensin II-induced aldosterone release. This will decrease aldosterone which will reduce urinary potassium excretion which will then raise the potassium serum
concentration.

In this case, the patient had both an NSAID (indomethacin) and an ACE Inhibitor (lisinopril) which led to his hyperkalemia. The ACE Inhibitor should be stopped, and the patient should be given IV Calcium Chloride to prevent
cardiac arrhythmias. IV insulin + glucose (or albuterol nebulizer) should be given to temporarily shift potassium into the cells, and Kayexalate or Furosemide can be used to increase secretion of potassium.

Dialysis is an option, but in this case, less invasive treatment should be attempted first, and if the patient continues to be refractory from initial treatment, then dialysis can be considered,
The patient should be admitted to the inpatient unit as he will need cardiac monitoring and IV medications and fluids. Once treatment is given for 3-12 hours, the potassium will decrease, and the patient will feel better, and
the case will end.
