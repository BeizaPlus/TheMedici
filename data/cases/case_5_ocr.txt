Differential: Broad differential but includes: Stroke (likely cerebellar stroke), bacterial meningitis, viral meningitis, sepsis, intracranial abscess, Herpes Simplex |, head trauma, Multiple Sclerosis, Tumor.

The patient has West Nile Encephalitis. This is a difficult case, and it is only testing your diagnostic skills. There is little treatment involved in this case. The patient is experiencing neurological symptoms, so a neurological
infection, stroke, intracranial hemorrhage and tumor should be high on your differential. A CT/MRI could help differentiate these causes. The patient then should have a lumbar puncture performed and CSF should be sent
out to test for protein, glucose, and cell count. With normal glucose, elevated lymphocyte count, and increased levels of protein, this suggests a viral meningeal infection. It is still a good idea to send out CSF cultures for

fungal, viral, and bacterial pathogens. Note that the West Nile Virus can show up negative in a CSF viral culture. Once you test for the West Nile Virus antibody and it shows up positive, the case will end. In your last two
minutes, you can perform/schedule preventive measures that the patient needs — a pap smear, mammogram, and the Tdap vaccine.
