Case Summary
Differential: Abdominal pain has a broad differential. However, the pain is somewhat localizing to the RLQ. This would suggest that the differential includes Abdominal abscess, kidney stone, Pelvic inflammatory disease,

Abdominal wall pain, and ectopic pregnancy.

With this patient's recent appendectomy and symptoms of RLQ abdominal pain, fever, tachycardia, and leukocytosis, this is highly suggestive of an intra-abdominal abscess.
Percutaneous abscess drainage is preferred as it avoids a possibly difficult laparotomy, the requirement of anesthesia, avoids wound complications associated with intra-abdominal surgery, and reduces length of

hospitalization.

This can be performed if there are no signs of necrotic tissue and the area is within reach of percutaneous drainage. Empiric antibiotic coverage should cover gram-negative organisms, anaerobes, and enterococci. Gram-stain

of the abscess can provide early guidance for selection of antibiotics.
