This patient has had a panic attack. She has a highly stressful job as an executive in a large corporation, Panic disorder is a diagnosis of exclusion, so medical problems that can cause panic attacks should be ruled out first,
such as hyperthyroidism, substance use, heart problems, pulmonary embolus, etc. Given that the patient is 26 and has no medical history with a normal EKG and labs, it is not necessary to do invasive and expensive tests such
asa Chest CT to rule out a pulmonary embolus. The patient reports that she has had similar attacks previously, so the concer for a cardiac event or pulmonary embolism is low. Acutely, the patient could be treated with a
benzodiazepine (optional) although first-line, long-term treatment for panic disorder would be SSRI therapy with or without concurrent cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT). The patient would benefit from both a psychiatry

and psychology consult before discharge, and can then follow-up as an outpatient in 1-4 weeks time.

physician who treats patients in a variety of settings. An official statement of your role taken directly from the USMLE: “ In the generalist role, you must manage your patient in both inpatient and outpatient settings.
Sometimes this may involve management in more than one location—initially caring for a patient in the emergency department, admitting the patient to the hospital, and discharging and following the patient in the
outpatient setting.” Essentially, you will see the patient in the ER and follow-up with them personally as an outpatient. Although this is not realistic, this is how you will be graded, so you should initiate SSRI/SNRI treatment in

the Emergency Room setting.
