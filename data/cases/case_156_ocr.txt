Case Summary

Differential: Heart Failure, Hereditary Angioedema, Kwashiorkor, Liver Cirrhosis.

The patient has Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome which is the most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in children less than 10 years old. An official diagnosis can be made by doing a kidney biopsy and doing an electron
microscopy which would show diffuse foot process effacement. However, biopsy is not necessary as a diagnosis can be made based on the clinical presentation and lab findings (high proteinuria and hypoalbuminemia).

Edema from nephrotic syndrome is caused due to damage to the glomerular filtration barrier which increases permeability which leads to edema. Most patients with idiopathic nephrotic syndrome have minimal change
disease which is typically responsive to steroid treatment.

Evaluation of nephrotic syndrome would include urinalysis to look for proteinuria, 24 hour urine protein excretion study, Lipid panel, electrolytes, complete blood count, and complement studies.

Treatment of nephrotic syndrome is steroid therapy. Empiric steroid treatment can be started which is typically oral prednisone, but prednisolone can also be used. Diuretics should only be used for patients with severe

edema such as those with pleural effusions and/or ascites.

Although the patient has dyslipidemia, a course of steroid treatment should be undertaken, and the patient should be monitored before any intervention is pursued.
