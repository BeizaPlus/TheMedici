Case Summary

Differential: Waldenstrom’s Macroglobulinemia, Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance, Solitary Plasmacytoma,

The patient has Multiple Myeloma (MM). MM is a neoplastic proliferation of a single plasma cell line that produces monoclonal antibodies. This leads to large amounts of copies of one specific immunoglobulin (typically
either IgG or IgA). MM is twice a common in African-Americans than in Caucasians. The etiology of MM is unknown. The disease will progress as the normal bone marrow elements are replaced by malignant plasma cells.

The diagnosis of MM is made by a bone marrow biopsy showing at least 10% abnormal plasma cells plus one of the following: Bone lesions (as seen in this patient), renal insufficiency (with a creatinine greater than 2 mg/dL),
hypercalcemia, or anemia. The mnemonic of CRAB can be used to show the most common clinical manifestations of MM: C: hypercalcemia R: renal failure A: anemia B: bone disease.

Urinalysis will be negative. The dipsticks will be unable to detect Bence Jones protein, Serum/Urine immunofixation and serum free light chain assays are not current orders on the USMLE.
The prognosis for MM is typically poor. This is a diagnosis case, and treatment is to simply consult oncology, give bisphosphonates, and give fluids. Other treatments are outside the scope of the USMLE. The treatments

‘outside the scope of the USMLE would include autologous hematopoietic cell transplantation, Melphalan, dexamethasone, thalidomide, Ixazomib, Doxorubicin, and others.
