Case Summary
Differential: Acute herpes zoster, Painful post-traumatic trigeminal neuropathy, Idiopathic painful trigeminal 
neuropathy, postherpetic trigeminal neuropathy.
The patient has classic Trigeminal neuralgia. The most common cause of trigeminal neuralgia is compression 
of the trigeminal nerve root (80-90% of cases). The compression is typically caused by a vascular loop. 
The criteria to diagnose trigeminal neuralgia include: having recurrent episodes of facial pain in one area of 
the trigeminal nerve; pain lasting seconds to 2 minutes; severe intensity; sharp/electric pain which can be 
precipitated by innocuous stimuli within the affected trigeminal distribution. 
MRI/CT scan should be done to rule out lesions such as tumor or neurovascular compression. The first line 
treatment for trigeminal neuralgia is Carbamazepine or Oxcarbazepine. Gabapentin can also be used, but this 
is second line. Surgery does not need to be consulted as medical treatment should occur and fail prior to 
involving surgery. The patient may improve over time and may not require surgical intervention. In this case, 
the patient will improve on Carbamazepine or Oxcarbazepine, and the case will end.
