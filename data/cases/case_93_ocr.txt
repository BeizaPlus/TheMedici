Case Summary

Differential: Heroin/Opioid withdrawal, Stimulant (Cocaine, MDMA, amphetamine) or hallucinogen (PCP, MDMA) intoxication, Cardiogenic shock, Hypovolemic shock.

This patient has a Staph infection likely from “skin-popping” which can happen when heroin users who have a difficult time finding veins inject directly into the subcutaneous or intramuscular tissue. These patients are prone
to infections and abscesses. This patient likely tried to lance his abscesses which led to a systemic infection. The patient became septic and delirious which led him to be brought to the emergency department for further
evaluation. He is in septic shock and should first be admitted to the ICU. Supplemental oxygen, IV fluids and empirical antibiotics should be given. Blood and wound cultures should also be collected, although they should not
delay treatment. Acceptable empiric antibiotics should have MRSA and anaerobic coverage and include Vancomycin in combination with one of the following: a 3rd or 4th generation cephalosporin (eg, Ceftriaxone,
Cefotaxime, Cefepime), piperacillin-tazobactam, ticarcillin-clavulanate, meropenem or imipenem. It is important to consult surgery for recommendations to determine if debridement of the patient's wounds is necessary. It is
also important to contact social work and psychiatry to help to arrange treatment for the patient's current heroin use.
