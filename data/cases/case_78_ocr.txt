Case Summary

Differential: Colorectal cancer, Renal Abscess, Chronic kidney disease, Hypothyroidism, Malignancy of other origin.

This patient has renal cell carcinoma (RCC). He presents with non-specific symptoms including malaise, weight loss, and flank pain and lab studies show anemia, renal insufficiency, and hematuria, necessitating further work-
up with imaging studies. Although ultrasound may be an appropriate first-line imaging modality, CT of the abdomen/pelvis is the most sensitive imaging study for renal cell carcinoma, and will show imaging features
suggestive of malignancy (septations, irregular borders, and contrast enhancement). Surgery/urology should be consulted to remove the malignant tumor. In most cases, nephrectomy or partial nephrectomy is used to get

tissue for diagnosis of renal cell carcinoma prior to treatment. Partial or complete nephrectomy is first-line treatment for early stage RCC. Imaging studies of the brain and chest should be ordered to look for metastases. Once
Oncology/Urology/Surgery are consulted, the case will end.
