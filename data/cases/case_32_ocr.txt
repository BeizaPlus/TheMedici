This patient is experiencing acute calculous cholecystitis, which typically presents with constant, prolonged right upper quadrant pain that radiates to the right shoulder. Other symptoms include nausea, vomiting, and
anorexia. The classic situation is fatty food ingestion occurs prior to onset of pain. The patient meets the “Four F's rule” which is fat, forty, fertile, and female which increases her risk of developing acute cholecystitis. Regarding
the need for antibiotics, the guidelines of the Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) recommend that antimicrobial therapy be instituted if infection is suspected on the basis of laboratory (more than 12,500 white cells
per cubic millimeter) or clinical findings (temperature of more than 385 ), and radiographic findings (eg, air in the gallbladder or gallbladder wall). Routine antibiotics are also recommended in older patients or those with

Note about imaging: Although a CT scan will confirm the diagnosis, this is not the first recommended radiological exam for the diagnosis of Acute cholecystitis. Abdominal ultrasound is the recommended first radiological

Murphy's sign is a diagnostic measure that suggests acute cholecystitis. When the right upper quadrant region is deeply palpated, inspiratory arrest occurs. Other diagnostic findings include: Leukocytosis with left shift
(bandemia); Abdominal ultrasound: gallbladder edema, wall thickening, and subserosal edema may be seen.

If the abdominal ultrasound is unclear, then a HIDA/cholescintigraphy scan is performed. Failure to visualize the gallbladder after 4 hours post-injection of the dye is diagnostic of acute cholecystitis.

Initial management of patients with acute cholecystitis include NPO status, supportive care (Intravenous fluids, pain control), and empiric intravenous antibiotics. The patient is admitted to the hospital and laparoscopic
cholecystectomy is usually performed within 48 hours.

Another subset of cholecystitis is emphysematous cholecystitis, and these patients are generally more toxic appearing. Gas will be seen within the gallbladder wall, and right upper quadrant abdominal wall crepitus is seen on
