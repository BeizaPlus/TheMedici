Case Summary

Differential: Cyanide poisoning, Hydrogen sulfide gas poisoning, Phosphine poisoning

The patient has carbon monoxide poisoning. The most common cause of carbon monoxide poisoning in the United States is due to inhalation of fire-related smoke. Carbon Monoxide causes its detrimental effects by binding
to the iron moiety of heme. This binding changes the hemoglobin and greatly decreased the effectiveness of delivering oxygen to tissues.

Lactate aids in differentiating between cyanide poisoning and carbon monoxide poisoning. If lactate is normal or mildly elevated, then this would be more consistent with carbon monoxide poisoning alone.

‘An EKG should be done to check for cardiac ischemia as this can occur in carbon monoxide poisoning. A pregnancy test should be done as pregnancy can change treatment. Note that a pulse oximeter will be normal as a
pulse oximeter cannot tell the difference between carboxyhemoglobin from oxyhemoglobin.

The patient should be immediately intubated and ventilated and started on 100% oxygen. Hyperbaric oxygen should be done in a patient if the patient has one of the following: COHb > 25%, COHb > 20% if the patient is
pregnant, unconscious; cardiac/neurologic ischemia; severe metabolic acidosis of pH < 7.1.

After treatment, the patient will improve and the case will end.
