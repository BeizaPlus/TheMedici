Case Summary

Differential: Meningococcemia, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, Streptococcal toxic shock syndrome, Toxic shock syndrome, Typhoid fever.

This patient has toxic shock syndrome due to her recent tampon use during menstruation. Toxic shock syndrome is a superinfection caused by S. Aureus, and recent tampon usage and surgery are the most common risk
factors. Toxic shock typically presents with fevers and a diffuse sunburn-like rash. Other symptoms include nausea, vomiting, hypotension and even diarrhea. The first step in management is to remove the source of her
infection, the tampon. It is also important to get blood and mucosal (vaginal) cultures and empirically treat the patient with broad-spectrum antibiotics, ie. clindamycin and vancomycin. In addition, this patient should receive
supportive measures such as IV fluids and oxygen. Patients not diagnosed and treated early are at risk of progressing into multiorgan failure.
