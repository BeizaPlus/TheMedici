Case Summary

Differential: Neurodegenerative dementia, Progressive supranuclear palsy, Parkinson disease dementia, Dementia with lewy bodies, Multiple system atrophy, Cerebrovascular disease, Alzheimer disease.

Normal pressure hydrocephalus is caused by excess cerebrospinal fluid that collects within the cerebral ventricles and spinal cord, resulting in increased pressure on the brain. A number of pathologies can result in normal
pressure hydrocephalus, which includes subarachnoid hemorthage, chronic meningitis, and other pathologies that prevent cerebrospinal fluid resorption. The mnemonic for normal pressure hydrocephalus is recalled by
remembering the 3 W's: wacky (altered mental status), wobbly (gait abnormalities), and wet (incontinence).

Patients with normal pressure hydrocephalus will demonstrate widened ventricles. Other findings, such as white matter lesions and cerebral aqueduct atrophy may also be present. A MRI is more sensitive in detecting normal
pressure hydrocephalus. A CT may also present with these findings, but it will not be as sensitive for this pathology. For diagnostic purposes, the patient can undergo a LP and drain 30 to 50 ml of CSF and then test the
patient's gait and cognitive function before, and 30-60 minutes after the procedure. The treatment for NPH involves placing a ventriculoperitoneal shunt.
