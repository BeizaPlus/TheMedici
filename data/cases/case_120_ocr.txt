Case Summary

Differential: Group C and G Streptococcal Pharyngitis, Retropharyngeal Abscess, Epiglottitis, Infectious mononucleosis, Viral Pharyngitis.

Group A Streptococcal (GAS) is the most common cause of bacterial pharyngitis in children. The physical exam and history are consistent with a bacterial pharyngitis. The patient does not have any viral symptoms such as
congestion or cough. The patient also has a characteristic papular rash along with tonsillar exudates. A rapid strep test should be done as this gives information quickly that will dictate treatment.

Penicillin/Amoxicillin are the treatment of choice for GAS. Alternatives include Cephalosporins (Cephalexin, Cefadroxil, Cefuroxime, Cefpodoxime, Cefdinir, Cefixime), Macrolides (Azithromycin or Clarithromycin), and
Lincosamides (Clindamycin).

Acetaminophen or Tylenol can be administered to treat pain and fever. Once antibiotics are given, the patient will feel better. The patient should be counseled to take the entire regimen of antibiotics to avoid developing

antibiotic resistance.
