Case Summary

Differential: Adenocarcinoma of the colon, Crohn's disease, Diverticular bleed, Infectious colitis, Acute mesenteric ischemia, Peptic ulcer disease, Ulcerative colitis.

The patient in this case has acute colonic ischemia (also known as ischemic colitis). Intestinal ischemia occurs when there is inadequate blood supply to the intestines either from acute arterial occlusion, venous thrombosis or
hypoperfusion of the mesenteric vasculature. Colonic ischemia is the most common form of intestinal ischemia and 95% of cases are due to hypoperfusion, as seen in this case. The colon (particularly the left-sided colon or
“splenic flexure’) is most susceptible to hypoperfusion due to its limited collateral blood supply. This patient has risk factors for ischemia (age >60, history of hyperlipidemia) and became volume depleted due to a
gastrointestinal illness (note: work-up of the specific Gl illness is not the priority in this case!) as shown by his low blood pressure, tachycardia, and signs of acute kidney injury. His symptoms of crampy, severe, left-sided
abdominal pain and bloody stool in the setting of dehydration should raise suspicion for acute colonic ischemia. The patient should receive IV fluids and broad-spectrum antibiotics and basic labs should be drawn
(chemistries, CBC, coagulation studies). An EKG should also be performed and his blood should be typed/screened in case a transfusion becomes necessary. Plain radiography of the abdomen will show pneumatosis (gas
within the intestinal wall) - a sign of advanced ischemia and CT will confirm the diagnosis of colonic ischemia. This case represents a surgical emergency and general surgery should be called right away. Once they are

consulted, the case will end.
