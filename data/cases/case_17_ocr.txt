Case Summary

Differential: Hodgkin's lymphoma, Cat Scratch Disease (Bartonella Henslae), Breast Cancer, Metastases, Non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, infectious mononucleosis, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), tuberculosis.

Hodgkin's lymphoma is a cancer derived from B-cells and is characteristically associated with the histological finding of Reed-Sternberg cells. Reed Sternberg cells are noted to have ‘owl-eyed’ nucleoli, surrounded by clear
cytoplasm. The cells are positive for CD15 and CD30. A lymph node biopsy will confirm the diagnosis. The patient will often present with painless cervical lymphadenopathy along with nonspecific constitutional symptoms.
Prognosis of a patient with Hodgkin's lymphoma can be determined after a PET or contrast-enhanced CT scan, which will show the dissemination of the disease. Staging is as follow for the disease:

- Stage |: Disease is limited to one single lymph node or one other site.

- Stage Il: Disease is within 2 or more lymph nodes on the same side of the diaphragm.

- Stage Ill: Disease is within lymph nodes on both sides of the diaphragm.

- Stage IV: Disease is outside of the lymph nodes.

Hodgkin Lymphoma is treated with chemotherapy and accompanied by radiation therapy in the early-stages of the disease. First-line chemotherapy treatment for Hodgkin Lymphoma is usually ABVD - Adriamycin,

Bleomycin, Vinblastine, Dacarbazine.
