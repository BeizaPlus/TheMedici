Case Summary

Differential: Peripheral vascular disease, Coagulopathy, spinal claudication, sciatica, embolism, Thromboangiitis obliterans.
Peripheral Artery disease can be remembered by the 6 P’s - Pulselessness, pain, pallor, poikilothermia, paresthesia, and paralysis. The patient has many risk factors associated with peripheral vascular disease: hypertension,
hyperlipidemia, diabetes, and smoking. Since the patient is experiencing intermittent claudication and not rest pain, the treatment is conservative. The main way to diagnose the patient is through ABI and pulse volume
recordings.

Normal ABI >= 1.0, Claudication ABI <0.7, Rest pain ABI < 0.4.

Arteriography would cause you to lose points on this case because this is an invasive test, and the patient should first be tried on conservative treatment. Conservative treatment is namely: controlling the patient's
hypertension, encouraging the patient to lose weight, smoking cessation, controlling cholesterol, and controlling blood sugars. If the patient had rest pain, the patient could be a considered for vascular surgery (surgical
bypass grafting or angioplasty).

The patient should be started on an antihypertensive agent, but lisinopril or other ACEI would be the drug of choice due to the patient having elevated glucose levels, and this would be protective for the kidney. Also, the
patient should be started on a statin as well as 2 glucose lowering agent such as metformin. The patient will checkup in 7 days.

There are a variety of antiplatelet therapies that could be tried, but aspirin is the most common and most tested. Starting on the patient on aspirin would be indicated at this point.

Since the patient has intermittent claudication, the prognosis is generally good. The patient has a good chance of improvement with medication and lifestyle changes.

All patients with Peripheral artery disease should have their LDL-cholesterol lowered to <100 mg/dL. Note that chronic kidney disease and diabetes can cause falsely elevated ABls. This is not present in this case, but itis
possible. This is due to calcinosis caused by glycosylation in diabetes.

We have also received emails stating that they feel that CT angiography should be accepted. However, CTA exposes the patient to radiation and iodinated contrast (can cause severe allergic reactions) and is more expensive.

Ultrasound is a better choice.
