Case Summary

Differential: Bladder cancer, Bladder stones, Bladder trauma, Chronic pelvic pain, Cystitis, Neurogenic bladder, Prostate cancer, Prostatitis, Urethral strictures, UTI.

Performing an ultrasound can also help support the diagnosis of BPH and screen for suspicious lesions that could represent malignancy, although imaging is not necessary in the diagnosis of BPH. Another important test to
obtain is a post-void residual, either by performing an ultrasound of the bladder or measuring residual urine from a straight catheter after the patient has tried to completely empty his bladder. First-line treatment for BPH is
an alpha-1-blocker, such as tamsulosin, which typically provides immediate symptomatic improvement. An additional treatment is with a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor, such as finasteride, which acts through hormone signaling
pathways to reduce the size of the prostate. However, this class of medication takes 6-12 months to provide symptomatic relief, which is usually why monotherapy with an alpha-1-blocker is the preferred initial therapy. Once
medication management is prescribed, the patient will feel better and the case will end, It would be incorrect to immediately jump to a surgical operation such as a transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) since this is
highly invasive, and the patient should fail medication management first before proceeding to a surgical procedure. Finally, it's important to counsel patients about lifestyle modifications to improve symptoms of BPH, which
includes limiting or avoiding alcohol and caffeine, as these are both mild diuretics.
