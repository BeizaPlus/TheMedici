Case Summary

Differential: Irritable bowel syndrome, Colorectal Cancer, Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's Disease, Ulcerative Colitis), Infectious (parasites), Lactose intolerance, Celiac disease

Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) is an idiopathic disorder that is defined by irregular activity of the gastrointestinal system. Symptoms include diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain, and bloating. [BS is classically noted to be
relieved by defecation. There are certain alarm symptoms that require further work-up, and suggest a more serious pathology, such as iron-deficiency anemia with unexplained cause, active bleeding in the gastrointestinal
tract, weight loss, and early satiety.

Irritable bowel syndrome is a diagnosis of exclusion, and other etiologies should be ruled out first. The Rome IV Criteria defines IBS as a patient with recurrent abdominal pain and at least two of the following:
-Abdominal pain improves upon defecation

-Onset is associated with changes in stool frequency

-Onset is associated with change in stool appearance

It is possible that the patient has other diagnosis as mentioned above such as colon cancer, infectious (parasites), lactose intolerance, or celiac disease. It is unlikely that the patient at age 29 has colon cancer, although it is
possible. The patient has had no sick contacts and has had no history of travel, so an infectious source is unlikely. It is possible that the patient has lactose intolerance, so a trial of lactose free diet is warranted with patients
with IBS. Loperamide should be given as an as needed medicine for the diarrhea and with a caution not to overtake this as this could exacerbate the diarrhea. Since the patient's symptoms are in the mild category, and they
are not causing significant impairment, conservative management is warranted. This would include exclusion of gas-producing foods (beans, onions, celery, carrots, raisins, bananas, apricots, prunes, brussels sprouts, wheat
germ, pretzels, and bagels). High fiber diet and exercise are also improtant. Smooth muscle relaxants such as Dicyclomine or hyoscyamine may treat the patient in the short term, but long term efficacy has not been
demonstrated.

In this case, conservative measures such as diet change and exercise should be tried first before starting on any medications other than loperamide for the diarrhea. If the patient comes back for a followup visit and continues
to have symptoms other medications other than previously mention can be trialed. TCAs (amitriptyline, imipramine, nortriptyline, and desipramine) and SSRis (fluoxetine, sertraline) are treatments for IBS which can be
attempted.

The main point in this case is to treat conservatively and avoid any invasive testing such as colonoscopy or sigmoidoscopy. The worrisome differential diagnosis can be neatly eliminated based on general lab workup, stool

sample, and fecal occult blood tests.
