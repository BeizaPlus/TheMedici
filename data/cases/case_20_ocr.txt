Differential: Third degree block secondary to: Infection (Lyme disease, viral myocarditis), increased vagal tone, progressive cardiac conduction disorders; Lenegre disease — fibrosis/sclerosis, Lev Disease (calcification),
cardiomyopathy, congenital AV block, hyperkalemia, hyper/hypothyroidism, iatrogenic effects.

Third Degree AV Block may be caused by an extensive list of pathologies, and results in a complete dissociation between supraventricular impulses to the ventricles. The most common cause of third-degree AV block is
coronary ischemia that involves the conduction system. It often presents with symptoms of syncope, dizziness, and hypotension. It is usually a bradycardic rhythm.

ECG will demonstrate P and QRS complexes that share no relationship. Patients can be temporarily stabilized with a transcutaneous pacer, but will require the placement of a permanent pacemaker.
Causes of Third-Degree AV Block:

- Pathologic: Myocardial ischemia, cardiomyopathy (amyloidosis, sarcoidosis), myocarditis (Lyme Disease, viral myocarditis), endocarditis with abscess, hyperkalemia, hypervagotonia

- _ latrogenic: AV nodal blocking medications (IE - Beta-blockers), post-cardiac surgery, post-catheter ablation

- Congenital AV Block
