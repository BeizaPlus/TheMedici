Case Summary

Differential: Acute Aortic Dissection, Pulmonary embolism, Pericardial tamponade, Esophageal rupture

This case is similar to case 124, but the medical treatment is very different. Right Ventricular Myocardial Infarction (RVMI) is typically caused by occlusion of the right coronary artery (RCA). The presence of hypotension,
elevated jugular venous pressure, and tricuspid regurgitation murmur is suggestive of a RVMI. RVMI tends to be associated more frequently with vagal symptoms, such as bradycardia, nausea, vomiting, diaphoresis, and

pallor.

Just like in case 124, the treatment is reperfusion. Cardiology should be consulted and an angioplasty with a stent placement should be performed (now known as percutaneous coronary intervention). This is the preferred
treatment. In the USMLE, you will have access to immediate treatment, so you do not need to do medical fibrinolysis treatment. If you do fibrinolysis treatment, you will gain points, but you will not get full credit as the
preferred method is angioplasty with a stent placement. The patient should be given ticagrelor or prasugrel (Prasugrel is not yet an order on the USMLE) as anticoagulants. Ticagrelor and prasugrel are preferred to
Clopidogrel, but Clopidogrel will still be accepted as this knowledge is out of scope for the test.

Aspirin and anticoagulants should be given, but beta-blockers, calcium-channel blockers, morphine, and nitroglycerin should be avoided in RVMI. Beta blockers can negatively affect the heart rate and contractility. Nitrate and

opioids can negatively affect preload and worsen the problem.

EKG will show that the patient has ST elevation in leads V4. Once treatment has taken place, the patient will feel better, and the case will end.
