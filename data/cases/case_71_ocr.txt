Case Summary

Differential: linflammatory bowel disease, Lactose intolerance, Irritable bowel syndrome, Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, Giardiasis, Bacterial gastroenteritis, protein intolerance, viral gastroenteritis.

This patient has celiac disease. She presented with gastrointestinal symptoms including diarrhea, steatorrhea (foul-smelling, floating stools), bloating and weight loss, suggestive of a malabsorptive process. Her physical exam
is essentially normal (with the exception of skin findings consistent with dermatitis herpetiformis) and the majority of her diagnostic tests are negative. Current guidelines recommend anti-tissue transglutaminase IgA (along
with serum IgA levels) as the preferred first-line serology test, as it is the most sensitive and specific for Celiac disease, but as of now this test is not an available order on the USMLE exam. However, a positive anti-endomysial
antibody, as this patient has, is still highly specific for the diagnosis. The gold standard for diagnosis is a small bowel biopsy showing villous atrophy, which will clinch the diagnosis in this patient's case. Once the diagnosis is
confirmed, the patient should be put on a gluten-free diet, which will improve her symptoms. 70% of patients have noticeable clinical improvement within 2 weeks of initiating a gluten-free diet. In addition to nutritional

counseling and a gluten-free diet, the patient should be screened for vitamin and mineral deficiencies, as these can occur due to malabsorption. A bone density DEXA scan should also be obtained in all patients diagnosed
with Celiac disease to look for osteopenia/osteoporosis, a potential consequence of vitamin D deficiency. Finally, the patient should be given a pneumococcal vaccine because Celiac disease is associated with hyposplenism.
