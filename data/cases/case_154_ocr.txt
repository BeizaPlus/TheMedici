Case Summary

Differential: Atrophic Vaginitis, Purulent Vaginitis, Gonorrhea, Chlamydia, Bacterial vaginosis, Vulvovaginal candidiasis

The patient has a Trichomonal infection caused by the protozoan Trichomonas vaginalis. Trichomoniasis is the most common non-viral sexually transmitted infection worldwide. Females are more likely to have Trichomoniasis
than males. The patient most likely got infected with Trichomonas vaginalis from her new sexual partner. The sexual partner should be treated for Trichomonas and confirmation of an Trichomonal infection in the sexual

partner is not required. The sexual partner should be given a single 2g dose of metronidazole.

The best way to diagnosis Trichomonas is to prepare a cervical wet mount and see the Trichomonas vaginalis moving on the slide under a microscope. Nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT) can also be done to diagnose, but

‘a wet mount is cheaper and more available.
The treatment of choice for a Trichomonal infection is metronidazole due to cost and availability. Tinidazole and Secnidazole (not currently an order on the USMLE) can also be used.
The patient should be screened for other sexually transmitted infections which include gonorrhea, chlamydia, and HIV. The patient should be counseled to avoid intercourse until the patient and their sexual partner are

asymptomatic and have completed treatment.
