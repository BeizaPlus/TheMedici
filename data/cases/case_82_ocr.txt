Case Summary

Differential: Giardiasis, Amebiasis, Viral gastroenteritis, Parasitic infection, Inflammatory bowel disease, Food poisoning (Salmonellosis), Hepatitis A, C. difficile infection, Cholera, Campylobacter infection, Shigellosis.

The patient has traveler's diarrhea from a bacterial infection (E. Coli, most likely enterotoxigenic strain) that he probably picked up from international travel to Central America. The patient can be started on an empiric
antibiotic (Azithromycin or a fluoroquinolone, such as ciprofloxacin) before the culture has returned and can remain on this since culture results demonstrated sensitivity to both options. An additional option to consider
would be an anti-motility agent, such as loperamide, which can help symptomatically but will not treat the underlying infection causing the diarrhea, so should definitely not be used as monotherapy.

Although most cases of traveler's diarrhea do not require antibiotic treatment and resolve within a few days, this patient has had severe (>6 stools/day), persistent diarrhea (>7 days) that is incapacitating and very much
interfering with normal planned activities. Other indications for antibiotic treatment (which this patient does NOT have) are fever and blood, pus, or mucus in the stool. In this case, the patient will continue to have diarrhea if
left untreated but will improve once antibiotics are initiated. It would also be important to counsel the patient about maintaining adequate hydration with fluids and electrolyte repletion.
