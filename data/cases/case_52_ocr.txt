Differential: Aortic dissection, Costochondritis, GERD, ST-elevation MI, Non-ST elevation MI, Pericarditis, Pneumothorax, Pulmonary embolism.
This patient has unstable angina. This is a medical emergency and an acute MI should be immediately ruled out. This is done by getting an EKG which does not show any ST elevations, although it did show ST depressions

which could still indicate ischemia. Other life-threatening causes of chest pain, such as aortic dissection or pneumothorax, can be ruled out with a chest X-ray. Although initial troponin was negative, another troponin should

be drawn 1-3 hours later to assure a negative result. An echocardiography should be done to rule out structural causes of chest pain and heart failure.

Standard of care for treating acute coronary syndrome includes dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor), an anticoagulation agent (heparin, enoxaparin, fondaparinux, bivalirudin), a statin, and a beta-
blocker.Symptomatic management for unstable angina includes IV morphine and sublingual nitroglycerine for pain relief. Cardiology should be consulted, and the patient should be taken to the cardiac catheterization lab.
