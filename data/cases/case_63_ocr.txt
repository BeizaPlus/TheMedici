Case Summary

Differential: CHF, Liver cirrhosis, Leiomyoma, Other intra-abdominal malignancy.

The patient in this case has ovarian cancer. The purpose of this case is purely diagnostic and not meant to test your knowledge of ovarian cancer treatment. The patient presents with generalized symptoms (malaise,
unintentional weight loss) but after performing a complete physical exam and finding an adnexal mass, malignancy (specifically GYN related) should rise to the top of your differential, as this is a diagnosis you certainly do not
want to miss. Basic labs in addition to CA-125, a relatively sensitive tumor marker for ovarian cancer (elevated in ~80% of patients pre-treatment) should be obtained in addition to a pelvic ultrasound, the first-line imaging
modality for an adnexal mass, followed up by complete abdominal imaging (MRI or CT scan). Because the patient also presents with dyspnea, a chest x-ray would be appropriate and it shows a pleural effusion, again raising
your suspicion for malignancy. Based on all these findings, surgical exploration to obtain a tissue specimen is indicated, as ovarian cancer is a histologic diagnosis but staged surgically. It is important to realize here that the
standard-of-care method of biopsying an ovarian or fallopian tube tumor is through surgery (not image-guided). Once the patient's tumor is removed and sent off to pathology for evaluation, then the case will end,

We have received emails asking why you do not give platinum based chemotherapy and get a cancer diagnosis from pleural fluid and not do a laparotomy. The answer is that Epithelial, germ cell, and stromal cancers of the

ovary, fallopian tube, and peritoneum are staged surgically. Surgical cytoreduction is commonly in metastatic ovarian cancer which is done via laparotomy.
