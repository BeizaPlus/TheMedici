Case Summary

Differential: Infectious arthritis, Gout, Osteoarthritis, Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), Psoriatic arthritis.

The patient has Rheumatoid arthritis. This should be suspected as the patient has inflammatory polyarthritis. Lab tests that can point to rheumatoid arthritis are a positive Rheumatoid factor (RF) and a positive anti-

citrullinated peptide antibodies. An ANA should be done to rule out lupus and other autoimmune diseases.
With the combination of radiological evidence, positive RF, elevated ESR/CRP, and negative synovial studies (for crystals and bacteria), then a diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis can be confidently made.

‘Treatment is a combination of a NSAID and a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD). The preferred line of treatment for a DMARD would be methotrexate. Other alternative DMARDs include: hydroxychloroquine,
sulfasalazine, leflunomide, Etanercept, Infliximab, and Adalimumab.

Opioids should be avoided due to addiction concerns and because DMARDs are more effective in treating rheumatoid arthritis. After several weeks, the patient will begin to feel better, and the case will end.
