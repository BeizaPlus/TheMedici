Case Summary

Differential: Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis, membranoproliferative glomerular nephritis, Cryoglobulinemia, Anti-glomerular basement membrane disease (Goodpasture syndrome), IgA nephropathy, Lupus.

This patient has had a poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN). The patient had an upper respiratory infection 2 weeks ago, that sounded like it was likely a group A streptococcal pharyngitis, but was not treated with
antibiotics. The most common timeframe for PSGN to develop is 10-14 days after infection, which was the timeframe we saw in this case. The patient had hematuria, edema, mild hypertension, low complement levels (low c3),
proteinuria, and a positive antistreptolysin-O. PSGN is usually self limited and typically resolves in weeks to months with a good prognosis. Treatment of PSGN is supportive, which in this case would include treating the
edema and hypertension with loop diuretics and an anti-hypertensive, such as nifedepine, plus salt and water restriction in addition to calling a nephrology consult. The use of antibiotics is controversial but points would not
be taken away if used. The difficult part of this case is to correctly rule out other causes of the patient's acute glomerulonephritis. This would include getting an ANCA, an ANA, anti-dsDNA, cryocrit, anti-GBM, and
complement (C3) levels. If these antibodies are all negative and C3 is low, then you can be assured that the diagnosis of PSGN is accurate. The patient should be followed up in 1 week time.
