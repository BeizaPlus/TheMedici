* Methylprednisolone / Prednisone / Prednisolone / Azathioprine

Reason: The initial treatment for lupus is steroids as this is an autoimmune disorder. We have received a question why Dexamethasone and Betamethasone is not accepted as treatment options. The standard
treatment of SLE is with Prednisone and Methylprednisolone, Betamethasone is typically only used in Lupus as a topical cream for rashes. Most studies have studied glucocorticoid treatment of Lupus with Prednisone
and Methylprednisolone. Yes, there are some studies showing effectiveness of Dexamethasone with Lupus treatment, but this is not typically recommended as first line treatment. For the USMLE, staying with the

standard treatment is ideal for the highest score.

Case Summary

Differential: Dermatomyositis, Drug reaction, Photosensitivity, Polymyositis.

This patient has systemic lupus erythematosus or SLE. She has a malar rash and joint pain which are classic signs of SLE. Other classic signs include fatigue, chest pain, mouth ulcers, skin lesions that worsen with sun exposure
(photosensitivity), and poor circulation in the fingers and toes. Her ANA and Anti-dsDNA are also positive. Many interventions can be done at this time, but itis first important to give steroids as the patient is in an acute
exacerbation. Also, it is important to consult nephrology (due to involvement of the kidney as evidenced by protein in her urine) as well as rheumatology. The patient's acute exacerbation will improve on steroid treatment
but may need additional therapy for management of her chronic disease. The patient should be counseled on her alcohol and tobacco usage. She should also be advised to use sun block.
