As this patient does not have any previous medical history and did not suffer from a recent viral infection, he is likely suffering from idiopathic pericarditis. It follows a brief, benign course after treatment with anti-
inflammatory medication. First-line treatment includes a non-steroidal inflammatory drug (NSAID) and colchicine. The treatment could be accompanied by a proton-pump inhibitor to prevent the development of
gastrointestinal ulcers, which was proven to be effective in recent clinical trials.

Invasive treatment is not warranted in this patient as the echocardiogram only demonstrated a small effusion. Effusions that are symptomatic or are considered moderate to large effusions (>2 cm) may be treated with a
pericardiocentesis or other surgical procedure.

If the patient develops refractory pericarditis that is unresponsive to NSAIDs/colchicine, then glucocorticoids could be used. Typically, pericarditis that presents as such is usually caused by autoimmune pathologies.
Uremic pericarditis is another pathology that has an alternative treatment — the empiric treatment is dialysis.
