The patient has gallstone pancreatitis. This is evident by the patient's symptoms of pancreatitis including epigastric pain which radiates to the back, nausea/vomiting, as well as diagnostic test results of elevated lipase, and a
CT scan showing inflammation of the pancreas. There are many different causes of pancreatitis, but the two most common are alcohol use and gallstones. The patient is also jaundiced and has elevated LFTs, further indicating
biliary obstruction, The Ultrasound shows gallstones in the gallbladder, and dilation of the common bile duct and the CT shows blockage of the pancreatic ducts (ampulla of Vater) which caused the acute pancreatitis. The
patient should be treated aggressively with NPO, admission to the hospital (inpatient or ICU), IV fluids, IV pain medications/antiemetics as needed, and ERCP to remove the obstruction. Once the patient's inflammation has
decreased, and she has clinically improved, the patient should be scheduled for an elective cholecystectomy with a general surgeon to prevent further gallstone obstructions in the future.
