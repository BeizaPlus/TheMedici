Case Summary

Differential: Duodenal web, Intestinal atresia, Intussusception, Meconium plug/ileus, Necrotizing enterocolitis.

This case is a case of malrotation of the gut with volvulus in a newborn infant. Malrotation occurs as a result of an arrest of normal rotation of the embryonic gut during organogenesis. Volvulus occurs when the small bowel
‘twists around itself, causing intestinal obstruction (responsible for patient's bilious vomiting, the most common presenting symptom) and potentially bowel ischemia and necrosis (due to the vascular supply being cut off and
why the patient's stool was red). The obstruction caused by volvulus can lead to severe dehydration and potential hemodynamic compromise. The important part of this case is to diagnose the problem quickly with
appropriate imaging studies (first with plain abdominal radiographs to rule out perforation then followed by either an upper Gl series or an abdominal ultrasound, either of which will show evidence of malrotation), consult

pediatric surgery once the diagnosis is made, and then treat the patient supportively with NG tube decompression and IV fluid resuscitation prior to surgery.
