Case Summary

Differential: Malignant hypertension, Acute intracranial hemorrhage, Cluster headaches, Cushing's syndrome, Illicit drug use, Intracranial neoplasm, Partial seizure.
This patient is experiencing hypertensive emergency, as he is experiencing elevated blood pressure: Systolic blood pressure > 180 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure >120 mm Hg AND evidence of end-organ damage.
In the case of this patient, he is showing signs of hypertensive retinopathy and acute renal failure with elevated creatinine.

Here are a number of manifestations that may present in an individual with hypertensive emergency: Acute myocardial infarction; Hypertensive encephalopathy; Intracranial hemorrhage; Dissecting aneurysm; Acute renal

failure; Flash pulmonary edema.

Hypertensive crises are often brought on by an exacerbation of underlying hypertension, frequently caused by noncompliance of anti-hypertensive therapy. Other causes include: medication side effects, recreational drug use,
pregnancy, anatomic abnormalities (atherosclerosis, renal artery stenosis), and endocrinopathies.

Patient care includes immediate admission to the hospital, IV administration of a titratable anti-hypertensive agent, and continuous monitoring of blood pressure, neurological status, and urine output.
A general treatment for the following presentations include

- Hypertensive encephalopathy: Labetalol

- Pregnancy: Labetalol

- Aortic Dissection: Labetalol + Esmolol
