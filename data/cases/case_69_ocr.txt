This patient has cellulitis, which is an infection of the skin and soft tissues underlying the skin due to bacterial entry via breaches in the skin barrier. The patient is a diabetic, with poorly controlled blood glucose levels (as
evidenced by his elevated hemoglobin A1c), putting him at increased risk for bacterial infection. The patient initially presents with classic signs of infection, including fever and leukocytosis, and exam findings consistent with
cellulitis (well demarcated erythema, warmth, edema of the leg). Blood cultures should be drawn prior to starting empiric antibiotic therapy. The patient should also have a duplex ultrasound of the affected leg, to rule out
deep venous thrombosis as the cause of his leg pain, erythema, and edema. Empiric treatment options for cellulitis in a patient without risk factors for MRSA should cover methicillin-sensitive S. Aureus (MSSA) and beta-
hemolytic streptococci species. Because the patient has systemic signs of infection (fever and leukocytosis), inpatient treatment with IV antibiotics is warranted. In addition to empiric antibiotics, the patient should also be
given IV fluids and insulin to decrease his elevated blood glucose levels. After a couple of days with antibiotic therapy, the patient will get better and can be discharged home with oral antibiotics and insulin, with follow-up in

2 weeks.
