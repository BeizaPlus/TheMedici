Case Summary

Differential: Fifth disease, Measles, Meningitis, Rubella, Scarlet Fever, Drug allergy

This patient has Roseola infantum (exanthem subitum), most frequently caused by Human Herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6). The classic presentation for Roseola is in infants 7-13 months old who develop high fevers for 3-5 days (can

often cause febrile seizures) that resolve abruptly, followed by the appearance of a macular rash on the neck and torso, spreading to the extremities. Infants are generally still well-appearing, active, and alert throughout the

course of the illness and treatment is mainly supportive (antipyretics for fever, ensuring adequate fluid intake, etc). The main point of this case is to recognize the clinical presentation and make the diagnosis without any

diagnostic tests and manage it conservatively without invasive measures.
