Case Summary

Based on history and the glucose level, this case can be narrowed down to the diagnosis of DKA. Other considerations are appendicitis, pancreatitis, intestinal obstruction, drug intoxication, hyperglycemic hyperosmolar non-

ketosis (HHNK).

The patient has diabetic ketoacidosis. The patient's glucose is excessively high in the 600s. The patient should immediately be started on IV normal saline and insulin, and transferred to the ICU for monitoring. Fluid repletion

should occur slowly as complications with cerebral and pulmonary edema may occur. Blood glucose and electrolytes should be checked q2hours until patient's anion gap has been stabilized (The best way to monitor for

resolution of DKA is disappearance of the elevated anion gap). After several hours with correct treatment, the patient will be feeling better, and the case will end. With the remaining 2 minutes, you can instruct the patient

about diabetes, counsel against marijuana use, arrange a pap smear, and give a HPV vaccine.
