Case Summary

Differential: Atrophic vaginitis, Cervical cancer, Endometrial cancer, Vaginal cancer, Bacterial vaginosis, Candidal vaginitis, Cervicitis, Trichomonal vaginitis, Pelvic inflammatory disease.

The patient in this case has atrophic vaginitis. Atrophic vaginitis is an inflammation of the vagina (and the outer urinary tract). This commonly presents with vaginal dryness, discharge and postcoital bleeding in
postmenopausal women (due to the thinning and shrinking of the tissues as well as decreased lubrication). These symptoms are typically due to a lack of estrogen, which leads to the thinning and shrinking of vaginal tissue.
This patient should first get @ pap smear, a wet mount, and chlamydia/gonorthea testing to rule out gynecological malignancies and sexually transmitted infections. While nonhormonal moisturizers and lubricants can
address vaginal itchiness and pain with intercourse in mild atrophic vaginitis, they will not address this patient's vaginal discharge. Once malignancy and infection is ruled out, she should be started on a low-dose vaginal
estrogen. Vaginal estrogens (such as premarin) should be used with caution in patients with a history of breast or uterine cancer.In this case, once vaginal estrogen is prescribed, the patient will feel better, and the case will
end.
