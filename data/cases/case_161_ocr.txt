Case Summary

Differential: Thyrotoxicosis, Panic attacks, Illegal drug ingestion, Carcinoid syndrome, Essential hypertension, Hypoglycemia, Migraines

The patient in this case has a Pheochromocytoma. Pheochromocytomas will secrete Catecholamines (norepinephrine, epinephrine, and dopamine). She has a 6 cm right adrenal mass which has been causing the patient's
symptoms of high blood pressure, headache, panic attack like symptoms, sweating, and dizziness. The majority of patients with pheochromocytoma will have headache as a symptom.

The way to diagnose pheochromocytoma is to measure the urine catecholamines and/or fractionated metanephrines. Once these come back elevated, imaging should be done to find the location of the pheochromocytoma.
The imaging choice is either CT or MRI of the abdomen/pelvis.

The patient's high blood pressure should be addressed. Phenoxybenzamine should be started which is a nonspecific, long-acting alpha-adrenergic blocking agent. This is ideal for preoperatively treating to normalize blood

pressure. Alternatives to Phenoxybenzamine are prazosin, terazosin, or doxazosin.

A beta blocker, particularly propranolol or metoprolol, should be started. It is very important to start this AFTER the alpha blocker is started to prevent unopposed alpha-adrenergic stimulation which could dangerously
increase the patient's blood pressure.

Surgery should be consulted to remove the patient's tumor. Once surgery is consulted, the case will end.
