Case Summary

Differential: Ventricular fibrillation, Hypoglycemia

This is an emergency case, so intervention should be done immediately before a physical exam should be done. The patient has a weak pulse, and CPR should be continued. PALS recommends CPR in patients with a heart rate
less than 60 bpm and signs of poor perfusion that does not respond to positive pressure ventilation. The patient does have a weak pulse. There is a risk of inducing ventricular fibrillation with CPR, but most intensive care

professional would start CPR in a patient presenting like this. The airway of the patient should be protected by intubation and ventilation.

The patient has hypothermia which should be corrected. The cold clothes of the patient should be removed, and a warming blanket should be given which will warm the patient. Hypothermic patients are almost always

volume depleted due to cold diuresis, so warm IV fluids should be given. The patient should then be given epinephrine based on the recommendation of Pediatric Advance Life Support (PALS) protocol.

After about 30 minutes of intervention, the patient will begin to recover and CPR can stop. After another 60 minutes, the patient will open her eyes, and the case will end.
