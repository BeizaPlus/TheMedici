Case Summary

Differential: Hereditary spherocytosis, Physiologic hyperbilirubinemia, Gilbert’s/Crigler-Najjar, breastmilk jaundice, hemolytic disease of the newborn/ABO incompatibility, biliary atresia, and G6PD deficiency.

The baby in this case has breastfeeding jaundice. Lactation (breastfeeding) failure causes jaundice due to inadequate intake of fluids and calories, causing slower bilirubin elimination and increased enterohepatic circulation of
bilirubin. The increased enterohepatic circulation also may result from reduced intestinal bacteria that convert bilirubin to non-resorbed metabolites. In this case, the mother's milk had not come in which led to a decrease in
milk intake which caused the patient's unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia. The first step in testing should be to determine whether this is indirect or direct hyperbilirubinemia. Once it is determined to be an indirect,
hyperbilirubinemia, hemolytic processes should be ruled out with a CBC (plus a smear and reticulocyte count) and a direct antiglobulin test for ABO compatibility (hemolytic disease of the newborn). Other causes of
unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia include hereditary spherocytosis, G6PD deficiency, Gilbert's syndrome, and Crigler-Najjar. The baby’s total bilirubin was at a dangerously high level, which can lead to kernicterus and

permanent brain damage.

The baby requires hospital admission in order to receive phototherapy treatment to prevent permanent neurologic damage. While undergoing phototherapy, the patient's total bilirubin should be checked every 4-6 hours at
first, then every 6-12 hours once levels start falling. After 2 days, the patient's bilirubin has fallen outside dangerous levels, phototherapy can be stopped and the patient can be discharged with a followup in 7 days. While in
the hospital, the baby should be supplemented with formula until the mother's milk comes in, but the mother should continue to breastfeed the baby every 2 to 4 hours to help establish successful breastfeeding, Educating
the patient's mother about breastfeeding is important.
