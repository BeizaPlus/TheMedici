Case Summary

Differential: Tuberculosis, Preumoconiosis, Fungal Infection, lymphoma, Pulmonary fibrosis, HIV

The patient has Sarcoidosis. Sarcoidosis can present in many different ways and can be diagnostically challenging in real life scenarios as it can affect many different organs in many different ways. However, in the USMLE, it

will be much more straight forward. Typical presentations can include: cough, malaise, cough, weight loss, dyspnea, and arthritis (typically involving the ankle and knees).

The presentation in this case of nonproductive cough, malaise, weight loss is also similar in HIV and Tuberculosis, so HIV and Tuberculosis tests should be done to rule these out. Chest X-ray will show bilateral hilar
lymphadenopathy and pulmonary function tests will show a restrictive pattern with reduced vital capacity and total lung capacity which is associated with a reduction in the DLCO.

First line treatment is glucocorticoids, typically prednisone. Alternative treatments include: Methotrexate, Azathioprine, and Leflunomide. There are other immunosuppressants that is outside the scope of the USMLE that are
alternatives.

After a month of treatment, the patient will feel better. A repeat chest X-ray can be done to verify that radiologically that the patient is improving,
