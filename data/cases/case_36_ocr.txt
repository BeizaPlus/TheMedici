Case Summary

Differential: Meningitis, Intracranial abscess, Epidural abscess, Migraine, Subarachnoid hemorrhage, Sinusitis, Encephalitis

The patient in this case has bacterial meningitis. The patient has the classical findings of bacterial meningitis which include nuchal rigidity, photophobia, and a positive Kernig’s sign and Brudzinsk's sign. The lumbar puncture
shows a gram positive S. pneumoniae. The order is important. It is important to get a blood culture, start empiric antibiotics, get a CT scan, then get a LP. Note that giving antibiotics should not change the lumbar puncture
result as long as the LP is within 4 hours of giving the first dose of the antibiotic. The patient should be given Ceftriaxone and vancomycin since this is a normal adult. This patient should also be given Dexamethasone which
needs to be given before or with the first antibiotic treatment.

Close contacts do not need to be treated in this case because the bacterial is S. pneumoniae. If the patient had N. meningitidis, then close contacts would need to be treated with Rifampi, Cipro, or Ceftriaxone.
We have received emails about why dexamethasone is the only accepted steroid treatment. Most of the studies that study bacterial meningitis neurologic complications use dexamethasone. Using a different medication that

has not been studied as well would be a mistake.
