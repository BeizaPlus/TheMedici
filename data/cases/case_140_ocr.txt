Case Summary

Differential: Intracranial tumor, Obstructive hydrocephalus, Choroid Plexus Papilloma, Subarachnoid Hemorrhage

The patient has Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension which is commonly known as Pseudotumor Cerebri. The cause of the elevated intracranial pressure is not known (which is why it is labeled idiopathic). However, obesity is a

risk factor as well as being female. The patient is obese and should attempt to lose weight which would improve her overall health.

ACT/MRI should be done to rule out an intracranial tumor or other gross abnormality. A lumbar puncture will show an increase opening pressure. CSF studies will be normal. The diagnosis can then be made of Pseudotumor
Cerebri.

The treatment for Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension is Acetazolamide which is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor which can reduce the rate of CSF production. Other medications can be tried if the patient is unable to tolerate

Acetazolamide such as Topiramate or Methazolamide.
