Case Summary

Differential: The differential for altered mental status in an elderly patient includes: seizures, bacterial meningitis, urinary tract infection, trauma, electrolyte disturbances, drug or alcohol toxicity, heart failure, metabolic

disorders (hypoglycemia, hypercalcemia, uremia, liver failure, thyrotoxicosis).

The patient has a subdural hematoma. The clue is that the patient had a recent fall and has been having episodes of altered mental status since that time. The patient is also at a higher risk for subdural hematomas due to his
advanced age and being a chronic alcoholic. Especially with falls in the elderly, it is important to check for head trauma. CT Without contrast is more widely used for the swift turn-around time for results, but MRI is more
sensitive in detecting intracranial hemorrhages. Time is of utmost importance in this case as bleeds may cause mass effects that may lead to increased brain damage as time overlaps. Neurosurgery should be consulted to
evacuate the subdural hematoma. Once neurosurgery is consulted, the case will end.

Subdural hematomas are a result from venous bleeding after blunt head trauma. The movement of the brain relative to the skull causes rupture of the bridging veins. Risks factors as noted above include: advanced age,

alcoholics, and patients undergoing anticoagulation.
