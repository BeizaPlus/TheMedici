Case Summary

Differential: Tuberculosis, atypical bacterial pneumonia (chlamydia pneumoniae, nocardia), nosocomial pneumonia, community-acquired pneumonia, tracheitis, bronchitis.

Pneumocystis Jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) is the most common respiratory infection in HIV-infected patients. It is often seen in patients with a CD4 count of <200 who are not being treated by antiretroviral therapy or receiving
prophylactic antibiotic therapy. PCP Prophylaxis is initiated when patients fall below a CD4 count of 200, and Trimethoprim sulfamethoxazole is the first-line prophylactic antibiotic for patients that do not have a sulfa allergy.

Candida vulvovaginitis is the most common gynecological complaint of HIV-positive women. Topical antifungal therapy such as clotrimazole is first line therapy, but if patient is experiencing recurrence or a resistant strain,
imidazole or flucanozole may be used.
