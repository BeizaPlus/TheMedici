Case Summary

Differential: Gastroenteritis, Pancreatitis, Perforated duodenal ulcer, Appendicitis, Ectopic pregnancy, Biliary colic, Ovarian torsion, Diverticulitis, Pyelonephritis

This patient has nephrolithiasis, commonly known as kidney stones. Her presentation of waxing and waning RUQ pain is very characteristic of nephrolithiasis and renal colic, plus her urinalysis shows microscopic hematuria, a
sensitive but non-specific finding. Although a CT scan is the preferred imaging modality for diagnosis, an abdominal X-ray (KUB) would also demonstrate a stone, as calcium oxalate (the most common type of stone
composition) is radio-opaque. It is important to rule out hydronephrosis from complete ureteral obstruction, as this would be an indication for a urology consult to intervene on the stone and either break it into pieces or
extract it manually. In this case, the patient does not have hydronephrosis or signs of urosepsis, so she can be managed conservatively with fluids, pain control, and an alpha-blocker (preferred) or calcium-channel blocker to.
facilitate stone passage. For pain control, opioids (morphine, hydromorphone) and NSAIDs (ibuprofen, ketorolac, indomethacin) are all appropriate options. The size of the kidney stone is also important to note; because the
patient's stone in this case is Smm, it will likely pass on its own without any further intervention. One study showed that 76% of patients with stones between 2-4mm passed them spontaneously without intervention. The
larger the stone, the less likely it is to pass spontaneously. This patient can be discharged home from the emergency room (does not require hospitalization) but she should first be counseled to increase fluid intake, modify
her diet to low-sodium, and strain her urine to look for the stone. Once it passes, it can also be sent for analysis to determine its composition, as this might affect future management or treatment decisions. At this time,
evidence does not clearly indicate whether patients with first-time kidney stones should only undergo a limited evaluation or a full metabolic workup to determine the cause of their nephrolithiasis,
