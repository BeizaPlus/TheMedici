Case Summary

Differential: Cholangiocarcioma, Metastatic cancer to the liver, Pancreatic cancer, Gilbert disease, autoimmune hepatitis, Primary biliary cirrhosis, Hepatitis, acute/chronic hepatocellular injury, choledocholithiasis,
cholangiocarcinoma.

This patient presenting with an ill-defined mass at the head of the pancreas, and is likely suffering from pancreatic adenocarcinoma. 90% of pancreatic cancers are adenocarcinomas, and the majority are localized to the head
of pancreas. It is a highly lethal malignancy, and the 4th leading cause of cancer-related death in the United States. The cancer is often linked to males and cigarette smoking. Symptoms include painless, extra-hepatic
obstructive jaundice, weight loss, and post-prandial epigastric pain.

Surgical resection is the only curative treatment — despite this, prognosis is poor despite complete resection. The Whipple procedure is utilized for resection of pancreatic head tumors, which includes removal of the head of
the pancreas, as well as a portion of the duodenum, the gallbladder, and some of the stomach. Once a biopsy is obtained and the patient's diagnosis is set, then itis important to consult the oncologist and the surgeons for
recommendations for treatment. Once the diagnosis is made, the case will end.
