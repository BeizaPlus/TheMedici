# Voice and format — how a LinkedIn post must sound and look

This is the rulebook for every draft. If a post breaks these, it is not done.

---

## Voice (non-negotiable)

Steve's voice is **founder-doctor-builder**: direct, philosophical, sometimes raw. Not corporate. Not hype.

- **No em dashes.** Use a period or start a new line.
- First person, conversational, direct.
- Short paragraphs. Plain sentences. One idea per line.
- Systems thinking. DNA / blueprint / baton metaphors. Long time horizons.
- Earned endings. No flowery language, no "thrilled to announce," no buzzwords.
- Occasional Ghana / Cedis reference where natural. Never forced.
- Teach, do not sell. The product is proof of a worldview, not the pitch.

**Banned phrases:** "thrilled to announce," "game-changer," "revolutionary," "in today's fast-paced world," "leverage," "synergy," "I'm humbled," emoji-stuffed headers.

---

## LinkedIn structure

LinkedIn shows ~2 lines before "see more." The hook does all the work.

```
[HOOK — 1 to 2 lines. A claim, a tension, or a question. No preamble.]

[blank line]

[BODY — short lines, 3 to 6 short paragraphs. Tell the small true thing.
Show the mechanism or the lesson. If there is a screenshot, point at what
it shows and why it matters.]

[blank line]

[TURN — the bigger point. Ladder up to the pillar. What this says about
medicine / building / legacy / scale.]

[blank line]

[CLOSE — one earned line. Optionally one question to the reader.]
```

- **Length:** 80 to 200 words for most. A demo/screenshot post can be shorter. A "why I'm building this" post can run longer.
- **No hashtag soup.** 0 to 3 relevant tags max, on their own line at the bottom.
- **One image** usually (the screenshot). Carousels only when the idea is a sequence.
- **First comment** can hold a link or extra detail (LinkedIn suppresses outbound links in the post body).

---

## Post types (pick one per draft)

1. **Screenshot demo** — "Here is a thing the product does, and why it matters." Pillar: usually MeWorld. This is the screenshots-inbox default.
2. **Build-in-public** — "Here is a hard problem I solved this week." Pillar: MeWorld or SAW/scale.
3. **Teaching moment** — take one real case mechanism and teach it the way the attending would (use `immersa-explainer` skill). Pillar: MeWorld. Pure value, no product mention sometimes.
4. **Worldview / why** — "Why memorization is the wrong model," "Why I document how I think." Pillar: Personal brand or Beiza.
5. **Operator note** — "Build it, document it, hand it off." Pillar: SAW/scale.

Across a month, aim for a mix. Not five screenshot demos in a row.

## Templates

### A. Screenshot demo
```
You can ask the attending "why is she hypotensive?" and it answers using the actual numbers on her monitor.

Not a canned response. It reads this patient's labs, this patient's vitals, and teaches from there.

[what the screenshot shows in one line]

Most med ed tools quiz you. This one teaches at the bedside, like a good attending would.

That is the whole point of MeWorld. You understand the body. You do not memorize it.
```

### B. Worldview
```
I trained in a system that rewards memorizing over understanding.

You can pass exams and still not see why the potassium rose.

So I am building the opposite. Real cases. A patient who deteriorates when you are wrong. An attending that teaches the mechanism, not the mnemonic.

If a 13 year old and a physician can both walk through the same case and both learn, the model is right.

That is MeWorld.
```

### C. Build-in-public
```
This week I made the attending remember.

[the problem in 1-2 lines]

[what you changed, plainly]

[the result the user feels]

Small fix. Big difference in whether it feels like a teacher or a chatbot.
```

Keep templates as scaffolding, not a cage. The voice rules win over the template.
