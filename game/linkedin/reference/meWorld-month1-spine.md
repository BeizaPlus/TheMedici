# MeWorld Month 1 — Umbrella Story Spine

The overarching narrative that runs beneath all 16 July posts. Each post is a beat. The spine is the thread.

---

I grew up in two worlds that both knew how to make someone good at something.

In visual arts, you had a sandbox. You made something ugly, looked at it, and made it again. Fast. In apprenticeship, you had a master standing next to you, his process visible right before you. You see him failing, correcting it in real time. There is something in a human that triggers when it sees a thought being worked out. Both worlds gave you the same thing: real time feedback. A room where being wrong was just the next step.

Then I walked into medicine and thought, how hard can it be, right? Oh boy, was I wrong!

Everything was routinized. Memorize this pathway. And the world wrongly represents brilliance as the ability to carry it all in your head. So I kept passing exams until I did not want to anymore. If I am really going to treat a human being, should I not spend more time understanding than memorizing?

For years, I thought that was imposter syndrome. But in the visual arts, I had no imposter syndrome. In medicine, I did. I carried it as if it were a character flaw.

The system was outputting decent, well-behaved slaves, practicing defensive medicine. But this is a human life!

But it was simpler. Medicine never had a sandbox. A safe place to fail, learn, and try again before real lives are at risk. That room just wasn't there.

Once I saw that, I couldn't ignore it. Mastery comes from doing, from failing and trying again, fast, in a safe space.

So I built that room.

### Distinctive Ana Moves Already Present

- **"how hard can it be, right? Oh boy, was I wrong!"** — Her exact phrase repurposed. Self-deprecating setup + punchline.
- **The two-worlds collision** — Same structure as her "artist vs. proceduralist" framing. Insight in the gap.
- **"That room just wasn't there."** — Negative space as the insight. Ana does this: "no management, no combat, no wrong answers."
- **"So I built that room."** — Five-word closer. The reader fills in the product. No pitch needed.

### Where This Goes Beyond Ana

Ana rarely goes this deep on personal cost. Her vulnerability is in process ("cliffs kicked my butt") not in identity ("I carried it as if it were a character flaw"). This spine is more emotionally exposed — the right move for medicine, where the stakes are higher and the trust required is higher. The vulnerability earns it.
