## Thu Jul 9  —  Pillar: MW  —  Act I: Problem

Why do fluids sometimes do nothing for low blood pressure?

Picture the arteries as a hose. Pressure is the squeeze of the hose plus the volume running through it.

Most low pressure is a volume problem. You bled, you are dry, the tank is low. Fill the tank and the pressure returns.

But sometimes the tank is full and the pressure still falls. The hose itself has gone slack. Sepsis or a toxin opened the vessels wide, and the pressure bled off into the periphery. **That is hypotension without hypovolemia.**

Pour in more fluid and you are filling a hose that cannot hold pressure. What it needs is tone. **That is when you reach for pressors.**

Same low number. Two different problems. The number alone will not tell you which.

## Tue Jul 21  —  Pillar: MW  —  Act III: Proof for Anyone

What does the heart look like at a potassium of 6.2?

Potassium sets the resting voltage of every heart cell. Push it up and the cells get twitchy, then sluggish, in a sequence you can read on the ECG.

First the **T waves go tall and peaked**, like tents. The cell repolarizes too fast.

Climb higher and the **P wave flattens and the PR stretches**. The atria struggle to fire.

Higher still and the **QRS widens**, smearing into the T, until the tracing turns into a slow sine wave. That is the heart about to stop.

The number on the chart is a warning. The ECG tells you how much time you have.
