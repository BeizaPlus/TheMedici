There is something strange about medical school. You spend years studying physiology, understanding how every system works, and dedicating almost half a decade to learning the details. Even with all this knowledge, the first time you walk into a real shift, you freeze.

That happened to me. I knew the material, but that was not the issue. The real problem was that I had never actually been in that room before. In school, every patient is just a case study. In the hospital, each patient is a real individual, a person with emotions who is looking at you for answers.

There is no practice round in medicine. Any mistake you make directly impacts another person's life and story.

You learn to be careful, but sometimes in the wrong way. You become slower, more hesitant, and overthink every move. You practice defensive medicine, becoming too afraid to trust what you truly know. You stop moving like someone with conviction and start moving like someone who is only trying to avoid causing harm.

These are not the same things. Mastery comes from failing repeatedly and learning quickly. But in healthcare, when you fail, someone's life is on the line.

So, how do you solve this?
