How to Read Axis Deviation
−30° to +90°

The electrical axis of the heart points downward and to the left — the normal direction of ventricular depolarisation from the AV node through the His-Purkinje system.

I ↑ Upright aVF ↑ Upright II ↑ Upright
Quick rule:
  • Lead I positive + aVF positive = Normal
  • Both leads "point toward" the QRS vector
  • No pathology implied
−30° to −90°

The QRS vector swings superiorly and to the left, away from aVF. Lead I remains positive but aVF becomes negative (inverted).

I ↑ Upright aVF ↓ Inverted II ↓ Inverted
Common causes:
  • Left Anterior Fascicular Block (LAFB) — most common cause; rS in II, III, aVF
  • Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH) — increased left ventricular mass
  • Inferior MI — loss of inferior forces
  • Wolff-Parkinson-White — right-sided accessory pathway
  • Hyperkalemia — at very high K⁺ levels
  • Pacemaker — right ventricular pacing
+90° to +180°

The QRS vector shifts rightward. Lead I becomes negative (inverted) while aVF remains positive. The heart is depolarising more toward the right ventricle.

I ↓ Inverted aVF ↑ Upright III ↑ Tall
Common causes:
  • Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (RVH) — COPD, pulmonary hypertension, cor pulmonale
  • Pulmonary Embolism (PE) — acute right heart strain; S1Q3T3 pattern
  • Left Posterior Fascicular Block (LPFB) — rare; diagnosis of exclusion
  • Lateral MI — loss of lateral forces
  • Dextrocardia — heart on right side
  • Normal variant — tall thin young adults, children
−90° to ±180°

Both Lead I and aVF are negative. The vector points superiorly and to the right — the "northwest" quadrant. This is almost always pathological.

I ↓ Inverted aVF ↓ Inverted II ↓ Inverted
Common causes:
  • Ventricular tachycardia — most important to exclude
  • Severe hyperkalemia — sine-wave morphology
  • Lead misplacement — limb lead reversal (RA↔LA swap)
  • Artificial pacing — biventricular pacing patterns
  • Dextrocardia + situs inversus
