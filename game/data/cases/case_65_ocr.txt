Suggested Order
Propranolol hydrochloride / Atenolol / Metoprolol
Reason: Propranolol, atenolol, and metoprolol are the first choice treatment for hyperthyroidism symptom
treatment. Propranolol in high doses can decrease T3 levels by blocking 5-monodeiodinase that converts T4 to
T3. However, this is slow (can take up to 10 days) and does not have a large clinical effect. Atenolol and
metoprolol also can minimally decrease T3 levels. The real clinical effect comes from the beta blocking effect
from the medications. Atenolol and metoprolol may be tolerated better than propranolol as they are beta-1-
selective drugs.

Case Summary
Differential: Anxiety, Atrial fibrillation, Early menopause, Mitral valve prolapse, Panic attack, Withdrawal
syndrome, Stimulant intoxication.
The patient in this case has Grave’s hyperthyroidism with a very clear history and classic physical exam
findings (tremor, sweating, pretibial myxedema, exophthalmos). The best way to treat this patient is to start
both symptomatic treatment with a beta-blocker (propranolol) and an antithyroid medication such as
methimazole or PTU. Both are acceptable, but methimazole is almost always the first-line choice with the
exception of the first trimester of pregnancy, during which PTU is preferred, due to methimazole’s
teratogenicity. This is why it’s important to order a urine pregnancy test on this patient prior to treatment.
Surgery and radioiodine ablation are alternative options to treat Grave’s hyperthyroidism, but it’s important to
know that patients with significant symptoms of hyperthyroidism (such as this patient) need to be treated with
antithyroid medications and achieve a euthyroid state before either of these other treatment options are
pursued.
