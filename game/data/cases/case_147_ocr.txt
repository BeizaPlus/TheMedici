The main differential is Graves' disease. Patients with Graves' hyperthyroidism have higher serum thyroid hormone concentrations. In Graves' disease
T3 is more elevated than T4. In postpartum thyroiditis, T4 is typically elevated while T3 is slightly elevated or within normal limits (like in this case).
Both Free T3 and Free T4 are elevated in this case, but the key is the degree of elevation. Free T4 is over four times the upper limit of normal (10.2
ng/dL vs. 0.8-2.4), while Free T3 is only trivially above the upper limit (680 pg/dL vs. 230-660) — essentially borderline. This disproportionate T4
