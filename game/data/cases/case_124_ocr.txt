Suggested Order
Atorvastatin / Rosuvastatin
Reason: The patient should immediately be given Aspirin along with a beta blocker and atorvastatin. In acute
STEMI, ACC/AHA guidelines mandate high-intensity statin therapy to maximally reduce LDL and stabilize
vulnerable plaques. Atorvastatin (40–80 mg) and rosuvastatin (20–40 mg) are the only agents that achieve high-
intensity classification at their recommended doses. Simvastatin cannot fulfill this requirement: its moderate-
intensity doses (20–40 mg) are insufficient for acute coronary syndrome management, and its only high-intensity
dose (80 mg) is subject to an FDA labeling restriction that prohibits its use in new patients due to an
unacceptably elevated risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis compared to other high-intensity statins. For this
reason, simvastatin is not an acceptable statin choice in STEMI regardless of the dose ordered, and only
atorvastatin or rosuvastatin should be selected.
Optional Order - Not Required - Does not affect grade:
Metoprolol / Atenolol / Esmolol hydrochloride / Carvedilol
Reason: Beta blockers are optional in this case. The REDUCE-AMI trial investigated the role of long-term beta-
blocker therapy after myocardial infarction (MI) in patients with preserved ejection fraction (EF). Traditionally,
beta-blockers have been a cornerstone of therapy following MI, primarily for their benefits in reducing mortality
and preventing future cardiac events, especially in patients with reduced EF. However, the benefit of beta-
blockers in patients with preserved EF has been less clear. REDUCE-AMI found that, in patients with preserved EF,
the routine use of beta-blockers after the acute phase of MI did not significantly reduce the incidence of
recurrent MI or other major adverse cardiac events compared to those not on beta-blockers. The USMLE can take
a while to reflect changes in standard of care, so this is optional for now. If beta-blockers are going to be used
Metoprolol or atenolol are the preferred agents for treating MI and oral is also preferred over IV. A frequently
asked question isn't a beta blocker contraindicated in an inferior STEMI until after revascularization and recovery?
The answer is that beta-blockers can be given within 24 hours for patients that do not have hemodynamic
instability, heart failure, hypotension, heart block, active bronchospasm, severe bradycardia, or a PR interval >0.30
seconds.
Suggested Order

Reason: Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) with Aspirin plus a P2Y12 inhibitor should be given to STEMI patients
without contraindications. Ticagrelor and Prasugrel are the preferred P2Y12 agents. Clopidogrel is also

Optional Order - Not Required - Does not affect grade:
Heparin, therapy
Reason: Studies are mixed whether heparin therapy is beneficial in ACS or STEMI. The AHA guideline
recommends heparin for patients with STEMI going to the cath lab, but this is based on consensus expert opinion
and does not come from empirical data. A case can be made to either include or exclude heparin, so this is an
optional order.

Reason: Cardiology should be consulted and an angioplasty with a stent placement should be performed (now

We have been asked why interventional radiologists isn't accepted as a consult. Interventional radiologists (IR) do
not perform angioplasty with stents (as part of percutaneous coronary intervention, or PCI) in cases of ST-
elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI).

Case Summary
Differential: Acute Aortic Dissection, Pulmonary embolism, Pericardial tamponade, Esophageal rupture
The patient has an ST-elevated inferior myocardial infarction (STEMI). ST elevations will be present in leads II, 
III, and aVF. The patient should immediately be started on dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT): Aspirin plus a 
P2Y12 inhibitor (Ticagrelor is preferred; Clopidogrel is also accepted). A beta blocker and atorvastatin should 
also be initiated. Cardiology should be consulted and percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) — angioplasty 
with stent placement — should be performed, as this is the preferred reperfusion strategy.
In the USMLE, immediate PCI is available, so fibrinolytic therapy is not required. If fibrinolysis is ordered, 
partial credit will be awarded, but full credit requires PCI as the preferred approach.
Regarding antiplatelet therapy: Ticagrelor and Prasugrel are the preferred P2Y12 agents, but Prasugrel is not 
yet available as an order in the USMLE database. Clopidogrel is acceptable and will receive full credit, as the 
distinction between P2Y12 agents is considered out of scope for the exam. Note that these agents are 
antiplatelet drugs, not anticoagulants — anticoagulation with heparin is a separate consideration in STEMI 
management.
Once appropriate treatment has been initiated, the patient will improve and the case will end.
