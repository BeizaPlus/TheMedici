# Storycraft Scale

Rate any story, script, reel, caption, or episode across 8 cognitive dimensions, identify the weakest links, and prescribe surgical fixes.

Synthesized from David Herman's cognitive narratology (storyworld theory, qualia) and Carolyn Handler Miller's dramatic structure framework (GMO, stakes, emotional journey).

**Core principle:** Emotion is the mechanism. Sequence is the delivery vehicle. A story works when the brain simulates the experience — not observes it.

---

## Step 1 — Score All 8 Dimensions

Rate 0–10 each. Be honest. A 6 is not a compliment.

### D1 — EMOTIONAL HOOK
*Does the opening trigger an emotion within 3 beats?*
- 0–2: Opens with exposition or neutral setup
- 3–5: Interesting but emotionally flat
- 6–8: Opens mid-emotion — tension, curiosity, or dread already active
- 9–10: Visceral jolt — audience is *inside* before they realize it

### D2 — QUALIA DEPTH
*Does the audience feel it sensorially — in their body?*
- 0–2: Tells what happened ("he was scared")
- 3–5: Shows external behavior ("he stepped back")
- 6–8: Evokes physical experience ("cold hit his chest")
- 9–10: Full embodied simulation — sweat, sound, smell, muscle tension, time distortion

### D3 — STORYWORLD COHERENCE
*Is the mental model the audience builds stable and believable?*
- 0–2: World is vague or contradictory
- 3–5: Rules exist but are inconsistently applied
- 6–8: Cause and effect are predictable — world feels real
- 9–10: World has gravity — audience starts projecting forward, asking "what next?"

### D4 — CHARACTER CONSCIOUSNESS
*Can I inhabit this character's mind — their goal, wound, and logic?*
- 0–2: Character is a vehicle, no inner life visible
- 3–5: Goal is clear but motivation isn't
- 6–8: We understand their reasoning even when we disagree
- 9–10: We *become* them — we're arguing their case inside our own head
> Always check for GMO: **Goal** (makes us watch) + **Motivation** (makes us care) + **Obstacle** (makes us feel stakes)

### D5 — STAKES WEIGHT
*What is genuinely at risk — and does the audience feel that cost?*
- 0–2: No clear consequence if things go wrong
- 3–5: Stakes are abstract ("she might lose the job")
- 6–8: Stakes are personal and concrete — tied to identity, love, or survival
- 9–10: Loss would be irreversible — we physically dread the outcome

### D6 — SEQUENCE LOGIC
*Does each beat cause the next — not just follow it?*
- 0–2: Events are chronological but feel random
- 3–5: Events follow each other but causality is weak
- 6–8: Each beat creates the conditions for the next ("because" not "and then")
- 9–10: Sequence feels inevitable in retrospect — each beat was the only possible outcome
> Chronicle: "The king died, then the queen died." Story: "The king died, so the queen died of grief."

### D7 — TELLABILITY
*Is this story worth telling? Does it deviate meaningfully from the expected?*
- 0–2: Nothing unusual happens — expected outcome, no disruption
- 3–5: Something happens but it's familiar
- 6–8: A genuine disruption — a violation of what should have been
- 9–10: The disruption reveals something true that the audience didn't know they needed to hear

### D8 — RESOLUTION RESONANCE
*Does the ending fulfill the emotional promise made at the opening?*
- 0–2: Ending contradicts or ignores the emotional setup
- 3–5: Resolves the plot but not the emotional question
- 6–8: Answers the dramatic question and delivers release
- 9–10: Recontextualizes everything — the audience's mental model shifts

## Step 2 — Calculate and Classify

| Total | Level |
|---|---|
| 0–25 | **Draft** — informational, not experiential |
| 26–45 | **Engaging** — holds attention, lacks depth |
| 46–60 | **Compelling** — emotional investment present |
| 61–70 | **Powerful** — memorable, shareable |
| 71–80 | **Masterwork** — changes the audience |

---

## Step 3 — Identify the 2 Lowest Dimensions

These are the revision targets. Don't rewrite everything. Upgrade the weak dimensions specifically.

---

## Step 4 — Map the 5-Beat Emotional Sequence

Check whether the content hits all 5 beats and label the target emotion at each:

```
BEAT 1 — DISRUPTION       What breaks from normal?
                           Target: Curiosity | Dread | Longing

BEAT 2 — EMBODIMENT       What does the character physically experience?
                           Target: Empathy (qualia — make them feel it)

BEAT 3 — ESCALATION       What makes it worse / raises stakes?
                           Target: Tension | Hope (alternating = suspense)

BEAT 4 — CRISIS POINT     The choice or moment of no return
                           Target: Fear + Investment

BEAT 5 — RECONTEXTUALIZATION  The meaning shifts — confirmed or inverted
                           Target: Catharsis | Revelation
```

---

## Step 5 — Emotion Targeting Audit

Check which emotions the content is firing. Not all emotions are equal.

**Primary drivers (highest narrative fuel):**
- **Dread** — anticipation of loss; brain can't stop running simulations
- **Longing** — desire for something unreachable; creates open loops
- **Injustice** — violation of what should be; triggers moral engagement

**Secondary amplifiers:**
- **Curiosity** — the open loop that pulls forward
- **Empathy** — identification with suffering or joy
- **Awe** — scale + beauty + unexpected = expands storyworld

**Weak emotions (use sparingly or reframe):**
- Happiness alone — no tension, no story
- Anger without stakes — unfocused, doesn't build
- Sadness without agency — audience pities but doesn't invest

## Notes on Application

- Apply to any format: short-form reels, MeWorld episodes, 99 Bitters scripts, captions, podcast intros, SAW copy, Beiza narratives.
- For MeWorld/clinical content: D3 (Storyworld Coherence) and D4 (Character Consciousness) are the highest-leverage dimensions — the patient must feel real, the clinical logic must feel inevitable.
- For 99 Bitters / brand content: D1 (Hook) and D7 (Tellability) are the entry points — if those are low, nothing else matters.
- For personal brand/HONY-style posts: D2 (Qualia) and D8 (Resolution Resonance) separate forgettable from shareable.
